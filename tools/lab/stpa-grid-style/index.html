    stpa-grid-style — drawing control structures in Leveson's style¶

    Living document, last changed 2026-10-17 at 7ac473fc.

    Contributor-facing. A styling lab, dated 2026-10-17. The published control-structure drawings (tools/structure-diagrams/render.py, Graphviz dot with splines) were judged "lots of elbows crossing each other, and hard to follow". This lab asks which way of drawing the same YAML inputs gets closest to the classic STPA control-structure figures, and measures the answer. It changes the visual form only: every candidate draws the same nodes, edges and labels from the same inputs. Nothing here replaces render.py or the published SVGs; the integrating session decides that.

    The conventions being aimed at¶

    These are the visual conventions of the control-structure figures in Nancy Leveson's Engineering a Safer World (2011) and the STPA Handbook (Leveson and Thomas, 2018), described from memory in our own words. No figure is reproduced.

    Confident (they define the notation and recur across the published figures):

    1. Vertical axis means authority. Controllers sit above what they control. Control actions are drawn going down, feedback going up. Position on the page carries meaning.
    2. One loop, two lines. A controller and its controlled process are joined by a pair of arrows: control action down on the left, feedback up on the right, as two separate parallel lines rather than one double-headed line.
    3. Straight, orthogonal lines. Lines are vertical or horizontal, with right-angle corners where a corner is needed. Curves are not used.
    4. Labels beside the line, not on it. The text naming a control action or feedback sits next to its arrow (control text on the outer left side, feedback text on the outer right side of a pair) and does not cut through any line.
    5. Plain rectangles, plain text. Boxes are simple rectangles with short names. Colour carries no meaning in the originals (the colours in this repo's drawings are a local addition).
    6. The two-column sociotechnical form. The generic safety control structure has system development on the left and system operations on the right, each a column of levels from legislatures down through regulators, company management and project or operations management, to the operating process at the bottom, with a few links between the columns.

    Inferred (consistent with the figures as remembered, but not a stated rule we can cite):

    1. Grid alignment. Boxes on one level share a baseline, and boxes in one column share a centre line, so most connections are single straight verticals.
    2. Horizontal bands between levels. The sociotechnical figures read as layers (government, regulators, companies, operations, physical process), which suggests horizontal boundaries for legal, organisational and technical regions. The originals separate levels by placement; whether they draw explicit bands or rules is not something we can confirm from memory. Here bands are an optional extra (bands: below), off unless an input asks for them.
    3. Few bends, and crossings avoided or made deliberate. Long links between distant boxes run round the outside rather than through the middle; where a crossing is unavoidable it is a plain right-angle crossing, not an oblique tangle.

    The measurements below are chosen to test exactly these: bends, the axis-aligned share of line length, crossings, label text over lines, loop pairs drawn straight and parallel, and grid alignment of node centres.

    What it does¶

    • Inputs: every tools/structure-diagrams/inputs/*.yaml (nine drawings) and the three generic tools/lab/stpa-diagrams/inputs/*.yaml (aid-loop, hub-spoke, governance), unchanged.
    • gridstpa.py: the new deterministic grid renderer (candidate grid). Python, PyYAML only, writes SVG directly. How it works, in order:
    • Each node sits in the cell (column, level); columns are as wide and rows as tall as their largest box, so centres align exactly.
    • Each pair of connected nodes picks the route with the lowest cost that passes through no other box: straight down; an L (out of a box side, into the top of the other); a Z (down, across in the gap between two rows, down); or a "corridor" round a column gap. Cost is 10 per bend, 6 per side entry, 15 per crossing with routes already placed, plus length. Every edge between the same two nodes shares the route shape, so a control/feedback pair is drawn as two parallel lines.
    • Ports on each box side and tracks in each gap are ordered, then adjacent swaps are tried while they remove crossings or overlaps. Downward (control) ports sit left of upward (feedback) ports between the same two boxes. Ports, side ports and gap tracks share one 16 px spacing, so an L or Z pair keeps a constant separation round its corners.
    • Labels are placed beside a segment from a candidate set (positions along each segment, two distances, either side), scored for overlap with boxes, other labels and any line; control labels prefer the left of the line and feedback labels the right. If a label cannot be placed cleanly, the gaps near it grow and the layout is redone (at most 12 times, keeping the attempt with the fewest bad labels). It reads the same YAML schema as render.py (nodes with level, column, kind, status, group, zoom; edges with kind, status; groups; views) and writes <name>.svg, one <name>--<view>.svg per view, and legend.svg, each on a white card like the published ones. Optional additions, all ignored by render.py and absent from the current inputs:
    key where meaning
    bands: [{label, levels: [..]}] top level shaded horizontal bands behind those levels, labelled in a left strip
    route: straight \| z \| l \| corridor edge restrict the route shape
    via: N edge, with route z: jog in the gap below level N; corridor: run in the gap left of column N
    label_side: left \| right \| above \| below edge preferred side for the label
    • lab.py renders every candidate for every input into results/build/<input>/:
    variant what it is
    dot-spline the published renderer's own dot source (render.py's emit, ids added), the baseline
    grid gridstpa.py, default costs
    grid-z gridstpa.py with a side entry costing 40, so edges enter tops and bottoms and take Z routes
    neato-ortho Graphviz neato -n2, nodes pinned at the grid positions, splines=ortho, xlabels (ortho has no edge labels or ports)
    elk elkjs layered, INTERACTIVE cycle breaking, layering, crossing minimisation and node placement seeded with the grid positions, orthogonal routing, fixed port positions, ELK's own label placement
    avoid libavoid (libavoid-js, the Adaptagrams router behind Inkscape's and Dunnart's connectors): nodes at the grid positions, orthogonal routing with nudging and hateCrossings, labels by gridstpa.py's placer (libavoid places none)
    control-grid positive control: grid positions; same-column and same-level edges straight, every other edge a Z with exactly two bends; pairs offset left/right; labels in a column to the right, clear of all lines
    control-random negative control: seeded random positions, random two-waypoint polylines, labels centred on their own lines, one forced node overlap

    elk and avoid get neutral ports (bottom of the upper box to top of the lower one, ordered by the far end, downward edges first), not gridstpa.py's, so they route on their own. Both are drawn with gridstpa.py's styles so only the geometry differs. route.mjs runs them. - measure.js loads each SVG in Chrome at its viewBox size, samples every edge path every 2 units, and scores it against the model (report.py writes results/metrics.md; the column meanings are at its top). It keeps the old lab's metrics and adds: - bends: total turning per edge in right angles (straight 0, L 1, Z 2; a spline counts its curvature), mean and max; - axis share: the share of all line length within 3° of horizontal or vertical; - label over line: text runs outside the boxes whose box (shrunk 1 unit) contains a point of any edge line, the measure the old lab lacked (its finding 6); - loops straight: both lines of a control/feedback pair one straight vertical; - loops parallel: both lines with the same number of bends, at a separation that varies by no more than max(8, half its median) along the middle 80 %; - grid alignment: levels and columns whose node centres spread by more than 2 units; - area per node. - sheet.js writes contact sheets: results/sheets/<input>.png (all eight variants) and results/sheets/<input>-dot-spline-vs-grid.png (baseline and grid side by side, larger). - inputs-demo/z0-sector-bands.yaml: z0-sector with bands: added, to show the rendering. Its band names and level ranges are illustrative, not a content proposal. Output in results/demo/. - results/grid-render/: gridstpa.py's output for all nine published inputs, with views and legend, ready for the integrating session to compare with docs/00-overview/perspective/diagrams/.

    cd tools/lab/stpa-grid-style
./lab.sh                      # render, measure, report, contact sheets
python3 gridstpa.py           # the nine published inputs -> results/grid-render/
python3 gridstpa.py -o OUT tools/structure-diagrams/inputs/z0-sector.yaml

    Installing¶

    Graphviz, Chrome, Python 3 with PyYAML and Node come from the system; the Node packages go into .tools/ (gitignored).

    cd tools/lab/stpa-grid-style
sudo apt install graphviz                     # dot/neato 2.43.0 used here
mkdir -p .tools/node && (cd .tools/node && npm init -y >/dev/null && \
  npm i playwright-core@1.56.1 elkjs@0.10.0 libavoid-js@0.4.5)

    gridstpa.py itself needs only Python and PyYAML. libavoid-js is LGPL-2.1-or-later and is used here only as a lab comparison, not shipped.

    Results, 2026-10-17¶

    Tools: Python 3.12.3, PyYAML 6.0.3, Graphviz 2.43.0, elkjs 0.10.0, libavoid-js 0.4.5, Node 24.15.0, playwright-core 1.56.1, measured in Google Chrome 154.0.8037.57. Full tables in results/metrics.md, raw numbers in results/metrics.json.

    Controls. On every one of the 12 inputs control-random scores worse than control-grid on bends, axis share, crossings, edges through nodes, label over line, label collisions, loops straight, loops parallel and grid alignment. The bend metric was also checked against ground truth: on control-grid the measured turning of every edge equals the bend count it was built with (largest error 0). Two metrics do not discriminate:

    • edge overlaps fails its negative control: random lines rarely lie on top of each other, so control-random scores worse on only 2 of 12 inputs, and better on 8 (control-grid reuses lanes and scores 23 overlaps). It does detect constructed overlaps, but a low count is not evidence of a good drawing.
    • loops control-left is at chance on random geometry, as in the old lab: control-random ties control-grid on 5 of 12 inputs.

    Findings

    1. grid is the closest to the target form on the measures that fit the complaint. Against the baseline it cuts bends per edge from 1.07 to 0.71, crossings from 43 to 10 (zero on 9 of 12 inputs), raises the axis-aligned share of line length from 0.127 to 0.995, draws every one of the 46 control/feedback pairs as parallel lines (baseline 18), and puts every node centre on its grid line (baseline: 26 of 53 levels and columns out of line, because dot keeps levels but not columns). The cost is size: 14 % more area per node on average, mostly height, since the gaps between rows hold the labels.
    2. Visual judgement (ours, from the contact sheets, not measured): grid reads as a Leveson figure. Authority runs top to bottom, pairs are two parallel lines with their labels outside them, and long links run round the edge. The baseline's curves cross the middle of the drawing and some labels sit between unrelated curves (z0-sector's "therapy plans" and "hub reports" overprint in the baseline).
    3. Where grid still falls short: 10 crossings remain, on z0-sector and governance (4 each) and aid-loop (2). On z0-sector the long "pumps, CGMs" link from companies to the AID system cuts across the middle; an explicit route: corridor, via: 0 on that one edge sends it round the left margin clear of the other lines (checked by eye on a scratch copy, not measured; the inputs were not changed). 11 label text runs still touch a line, on 6 inputs; the placer reports them as the least bad position, and growing the gaps did not free them.
    4. grid-z (side entries made expensive) has the fewest labels over lines (1/254) but nearly twice the bends (1.34) and puts control right of feedback on 9 of 46 pairs: forcing tops and bottoms makes nested Z pairs that the port order cannot always keep control-left. Whether arrows entering a box's side look acceptable is a judgement call; the numbers favour grid.
    5. neato-ortho (Graphviz with the same pinned positions) matches grid on bends, axis share and crossings, so the router is not the gain on those; the gain is in the parts Graphviz ortho cannot do. It ignores ports (control-left 36/46, parallel 26/46), draws 3 edge pairs on top of each other, and its xlabels land on lines: 86 of 247 label runs, against 11 for grid.
    6. elk does not keep the grid. Its INTERACTIVE strategies are seeded by the positions but still re-place nodes (26 of 53 levels and columns out of line) and route edge labels as dummy nodes, giving staircase routes: 5.04 bends per edge and 362 crossings. It was the worst candidate on every shape measure. Not pursued further.
    7. avoid (libavoid with the grid positions) is clean on crossings (14) and never draws through a box, but its nudging separates parallel runs unevenly and it doubles the bends (1.77): only 15 of 46 pairs parallel. A general-purpose connector router does not know the control-left/feedback-right pair rule; grid builds it in.
    8. The old lab's control-grid positive control was a useful hint: a deterministic placement plus a small amount of STPA-specific routing beats the general layout engines here.

    Recommendation. Adopt gridstpa.py (variant grid) as the renderer for the control-structure drawings, replacing the Graphviz spline renderer, after the integrating session has looked at results/grid-render/ beside the published SVGs. It reads the same inputs unchanged and needs no tool beyond Python and PyYAML. Use route: hints only for the few edges that still cross (z0-sector's "pumps, CGMs" first) and leave bands: off until the content work decides what the bands should say.

    What this does not measure:

    • How readable a reader finds a drawing; the visual judgements above are ours.
    • Whether a label is unambiguously next to its own line. A label can sit clear of every line and still be nearer to a neighbour's line than to its own; nothing here scores that.
    • Lines crossing group borders or band boundaries, and group labels under lines (counted as label over line, not separately).
    • Text width: gridstpa.py sizes boxes and labels from Helvetica advance widths. Chrome here rendered with Arimo/Nimbus Sans (metric-compatible); a viewer with a wider fallback font could see text overrun its box.
    • Stability under edits. Node positions are fixed by (column, level), so adding an edge cannot move a node, but it can change other edges' routes and label positions; not measured.
    • Larger structures. The inputs have 5 to 10 nodes and 7 to 21 edges; the renderer's swap search is quadratic in edges and was not tried beyond that.
    • Dark mode: every drawing sits on its own white card, as the published ones do; no dark variant.
