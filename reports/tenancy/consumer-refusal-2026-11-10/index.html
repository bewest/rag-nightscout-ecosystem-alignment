    Realtime consumer refusal under evaluator timer churn¶

    Living document, last changed 2026-11-10 at de1643e1b.

    Contributor-facing measurement record, captured 2026-11-10. Not edited after capture. Lane codex/consumer-refusal-20261110; local, unpushed. Settles the suspected pre-existing defect reported by the activity-eligibility lane ("evaluator timers at 150 ms make the realtime consumer refuse changed output on unmodified b70ad83f").

    Verdict¶

    Reproduced, mechanism diagnosed. The refusal is a product behaviour, and it also occurs at the deployed timer intervals. The reported outcome "nothing delivered within 10 s" depends on the native fixture's consumer timers (poll/retry 1,000,000 ms), which never re-drive a refused consumer. With the deployed consumer timers (1,000 ms), every delivery completed (14/14). Fixed on the lane branch; a second, non-throwing path under the same churn is left open as a decision (below).

    Mechanism (at b70ad83f)¶

    1. Every evaluator cycle, time-only included, publishes a new last with outputRevision: ++state.revision (lib/server/serve/evaluator-runtime.js:82). The recovery and time timers call all() directly (evaluator-runtime.js:169-170) and do not wake the consumer. Only the explicit acquire/tick wrapper wakes it (lib/server/serve/evaluator-preview.js:49-52).
    2. producer.select captures observed = current(tenant), then checks unchanged(), which requires the same runtimeId/outputRevision/inputCheckpoint (lib/tenant-notifications/producer.js:21-28). It checks inside the store transaction after the suppression read (producer.js:32) and again after commit (producer.js:46). A publication in that window throws NOTIFICATION_PRODUCER_STALE. Measured selection time is 18-55 ms. The two timers fire together, and each burst publishes two cycles per tenant within about 40 ms.
    3. Two paths reach it:
    4. drain: lib/server/serve/evaluator-consumers.js:37 producer.select throws. The catch at evaluator-consumers.js:49-53 records refused / EVALUATOR_CONSUMER_REFUSED and does not re-enqueue.
    5. attempt: notifier.retry → attempt commits the request as attempting with attempt_until = at + attemptTimeoutMs (lib/tenant-notifications/component.js:79). eligible() then calls selectCycle (component.js:31, :38), which throws, and the error escapes attempt with the row still attempting. due() returns an attempting row only after attempt_until (lib/tenant-notifications/postgres.js:125).
    6. Nothing re-drives the consumer until its own poll/retry timer fires (evaluator-consumers.js:74-75). In the native fixture those are 1,000,000 ms (tests/fixtures/evaluator-consumers-native.js:83), hence "stuck attempting, nothing delivered".

    Diagnosed with temporary probes (since removed) that wrote to a scratch file: the swallowed error in the drain catch, producer.select start/duration, and evaluator cycle start/end, publication and timer fire. Two refusals per failing run, both NOTIFICATION_PRODUCER_STALE: one at producer.js:46 via component.js:38 ← :83, one at producer.js:32 via the drain.

    Why the reported failure looked interval-bound: the failure is phase-locked to the test's own sequence. The timers start at boot readiness. The test's acquire+wake consumer work runs from about +180 ms to +330 ms after that. Any interval up to about 330 ms puts the first timer burst inside it, and 600 ms or 1000 ms at the same phase never does. The phase-0 passes at 600/1000 ms therefore do not show that production intervals are safe. Random-phase runs at 1000 ms do reach the refusal (below).

    Not fixed: the freshness gate under churn (decision)¶

    With the fix, refusals were 0 in every run, but delivery of the changed reading under 150 ms churn with the fixture's manual consumer timers was not guaranteed:

    fix, fixture consumer timers delivered
    150 ms phase 0, deadline 10 s (†) 5/6
    300 ms phase 0, deadline 10 s (†) 3/3
    150 ms random phase, deadline 10 s (†) 3/4
    150 ms phase 0 / 300 ms phase 0, at the moment of flush (no probe) 3/5, 3/3

    Mechanism of the remainder (read from the traces): eligible() compares two selections taken either side of the suppression check and requires an equal outputRevision (component.js:38-43). A time-only publication between them returns false, so attempt cancels the request (component.js:83) or defers it with the row left attempting until its deadline (component.js:92). This is a gate, not an error, and it is intentional ("a config/allowlist change while the suppression read was pending cannot admit old work"). The consumer's own timer re-drives it. The evaluator's timer publications do not.

    Options for the maintainer (multitenant only; single tenant does not run this code):

    • (a) Timer publications wake the consumer. evaluator-runtime.js timers notify the consumer as the explicit acquire/tick wrapper already does (evaluator-preview.js:49-52). Every published output then gets a consumer pass, which matches the realtime-cycle policy. The gate stays strict. Cost: one coalesced drain per publication per tenant (unmeasured).
    • (b) Relax the gate. Compare emitted effects instead of outputRevision for time-only republishes. This changes a freshness rule that guards against admitting old work.

    Recommendation, if asked: (a). It keeps the safety gate unchanged and the clinical/delivery semantics as designed, and it removes the dependence on the consumer's poll interval for timer-driven output. Its throughput cost is unmeasured.

    Operator-visible effect (multitenant preview, not in any release)¶

    • Measured at the deployed intervals: consumer health intermittently reports EVALUATOR_CONSUMER_REFUSED (5/10 runs), and delivery is delayed by up to one consumer poll (all deliveries within 305 ms at 1000 ms timers; up to 2.9 s under 150 ms churn).
    • Read-derived, not observed in 14 deployed-interval runs: a request left attempting by the attempt path waits for attemptTimeoutMs (10 s deployed) before due() returns it.
    • With consumer poll/retry intervals much longer than the evaluator's, a refused or deferred handoff waits for the consumer's next poll; the fixture's 1,000,000 ms is the extreme case.

    Proposed register entry (no id allocated)¶

    Realtime consumer refuses when the evaluator's own timers publish during a selection. Multitenant evaluator preview (#8898 workspace); not released. producer.select treats a newer evaluator output published between observing and committing as NOTIFICATION_PRODUCER_STALE (lib/tenant-notifications/producer.js:32,46 at b70ad83f). The coordinator records EVALUATOR_CONSUMER_REFUSED without re-driving (lib/server/serve/evaluator-consumers.js:49-53). Inside an attempt, the error escapes after the request is committed attempting (lib/tenant-notifications/component.js:79,83). The evaluator's timers publish without waking the consumer (lib/server/serve/evaluator-runtime.js:169-170), so the handoff waits for the consumer's next poll, or for attemptTimeoutMs for an attempting row. Basis: reproduced (native, 150 ms timers 3/3 refused; deployed 1000 ms intervals, refusal in 5/10 random-phase runs, delivery delayed by at most one poll; deterministic controlled-interleaving unit pair red/green). Status: fixed on codex/consumer-refusal-20261110 1adb17f8 (bounded reselect); not merged. Related, open: freshness gate in eligible() defers or cancels under churn with no wake from timer publications; decision (a)/(b) above.

    Evidence: this record; lane handoff externals/work/HANDOFF-consumer-refusal-20261110.md (local).

    Addendum 2026-11-10: revision-on-content-change stopped at the reader inventory¶

    Decision evaluator-revision-on-content-change (alignment main b9e78338). Lane head unchanged at 1adb17f8, tree clean. No code was changed: one reader relies on the per-cycle bump, so the lane stopped and reported, as instructed.

    What changes between cycles over unchanged data (reproduced natively; actual evaluator role, one synthetic reading, tick/acquire over IPC, including one tick 61 s later): only outputRevision, result.evaluationRevision, trigger and result.now. checkpoint, progress, emitted, requested and properties (bgnow, buckets, direction) were identical. A content digest over those five fields would therefore hold the revision on every timer cycle.

    Readers of the evaluator's last / outputRevision (at 1adb17f8):

    reader use per-cycle bump
    lib/tenant-notifications/producer-state.js:57-68 each new [identity, runtimeId, outputRevision, inputCheckpoint] is a fresh cycle: re-runs legacy notifications.process, which re-emits the active unsnoozed alarm relies on it
    tests/evaluator-consumers.integration.test.js:44-45 M5_CONSUMER_NEW_RT_CYCLE a tick over identical data must deliver a second realtime event relies on it
    activity lane tests/tenant-activity.integration.test.js:128 (07981b55) ACTIVITY_B_UNAFFECTED B's health outputRevision must advance by more than 2 over the timer window relies on it (test oracle)
    lib/tenant-notifications/producer.js:36-39 unchanged() runtimeId/outputRevision/inputCheckpoint equality and result object identity no; benefits only if an unchanged cycle keeps the same frozen last object
    lib/tenant-notifications/producer.js:60-61, component.js:22-24,39-40,125 the producer's own store revision, not the evaluator's no (indirect: it moves when the producer state moves)
    lib/tenant-alarms/cycle.js:11-13,24 shape validation; the revision must not change within one cycle no
    lib/server/serve/evaluator-runtime.js:146,149-150 health report, last() no (reporting)

    lib/server/realtime/runtime.js keeps its own cell.last and does not read the evaluator's revision.

    Why the reliance is behavioural. Legacy single-tenant Nightscout re-emits the highest unsnoozed alarm on every processing cycle (lib/notifications.js:131-165, emitNotification at :156), gated only by the ack silence window (:63). Processing runs on every heartbeat tick (lib/server/bootevent.js:537-540; heartbeat default 60 s, lib/settings.js:38) and on each data-received event. The multitenant producer reproduces that repeat by treating each evaluator cycle as fresh. Under the decision as worded, a time-only cycle with identical output keeps its revision, so fresh is false and an unacknowledged alarm is delivered once per content change, not once per cycle. That is a change from single-tenant behaviour. Read-derived: the client-side effect of fewer repeats (e.g. a client that connects after the first delivery) was not measured.

    Related measured fact: at the deployed timeIntervalMs 1000, the multitenant consumer re-delivers an active alarm about once per second per tenant, against legacy's once per heartbeat (60 s default).

    Questions for the maintainer before implementation:

    1. Should alarm repetition stay per cycle (as in single tenant), become per content change, or follow a separate repeat clock (e.g. the legacy heartbeat interval)?
    2. If repetition is kept, should the producer's fresh key be decoupled from the evaluator revision (for example, a repeat tick counter driven by a heartbeat), so the revision can still move only on content change?

    Addendum 2026-11-10 (second): revision on content change and the alarm repeat clock¶

    Decisions evaluator-revision-on-content-change and alarm-repeat-clock (alignment main fee6e40e). Lane codex/consumer-refusal-20261110: 1adb17f8 → 1d39c924 (telemetry) → 956d0940 (behaviour). PostgreSQL 16.15 disposable container (same digest), removed afterwards.

    What changed (956d0940)¶

    • Evaluator (lib/server/serve/evaluator-runtime.js): content is a canonical SHA-256 digest of checkpoint, progress, emitted, requested and properties (exported CONTENT, contentDigest). now, evaluationRevision and trigger are per-cycle metadata. Identical content keeps outputRevision and the same frozen last object. A new revision calls onRevision(tenant). Health adds cycles (from 1d39c924).
    • Producer (lib/tenant-notifications/producer-state.js, producer.js): when the current output requests any notification (armed), the legacy processing of that output runs again once at >= repeatAt, at time at. repeatAt = at + heartbeat after each processing. heartbeat is the tenant's single-site setting in seconds (environment.js:13 CLIENT_KEYS; the owner catalogue carries it too). If it is unset or not a positive number, the default is lib/settings.js:38 (60). Silence is decided by notifications.js:63 as before, and the suppression check (component.js:35) still applies at handoff.
    • Consumer (lib/server/serve/evaluator-consumers.js, evaluator-preview.js): revised(tenant) wakes a tenant with a pending request, an armed repeat clock or a current output that requests a notification. A one-shot wake fires at the producer's repeatAt, with a 1 s floor for an overdue time. Health adds passes, selections and repeatAt (the first two from 1d39c924). The stale-selection retry from 1adb17f8 is kept.

    Content between cycles over unchanged data (reproduced, before this change)¶
