    Bringing a controller onto a hub: register it, hold a lease, receive commands and report outcomes¶

    Living document, last changed 2026-10-26 at 36487a5dd.

    Contributor-facing. Living guide, current at main 127f69e1 (2026-10-18). Uses the platform specifications; terms are in the glossary. Status: the specifications are proposed and not implemented; until HUB-REG-CRM is, each step is checked against its conformance case, not run against a hub.

    This guide is for a developer bringing an automated insulin delivery controller, or a controller app, onto a Nightscout hub that implements the platform specifications. It follows one fictional controller, Example TestController, whose registration is the synthetic fixture specs/sync/registrations/fixtures/example-testcontroller.yaml, from the first request to the outcome of a remote command.

    Each step names the rule it follows and a conformance case under specs/sync/conformance/ that shows it. The examples are copied from those cases by python3 -m tools.nsschema.guide_examples and never typed by hand, so they name the cases' own fictional products (Example ControllerA, NegotiationController) where TestController would stand. Times in the command cases are seconds from the start of the case. Paths and permission names are proposals, as the specifications say.

    1. What the hub does and never does¶

    The hub records, routes and refuses. It never decides whether a change to therapy is safe, never alters what a sender asked, and never doses (D18). Your controller validates and enacts.

    the hub your controller
    accepts or refuses each element of your registration, and stores typed records for what it accepts (registration §5) writes everything the hub does not accept in today's document shapes, its carriers (registration §6)
    grants each (channel, role) to one live device instance and records overlaps (registration §9) keeps running when a role is refused, and writes that channel's records as carriers
    checks a command's channel, routing, expiry, size and the sender's permission, and nothing else (CM5) checks the payload hash, the expiry by its own clock, its own limits and the doses and carbs since the command was made (CM10, CM23)
    records the command, its state and the outcome you report (CM1, CM7) decides each commandId once, across restarts (CM21), and reports what it did
    reports overlapping intents as the controller reported them (state and intent S1) arbitrates between the person, the algorithm, caregivers and agents acting on one pump

    The control-loop analysis lists the entries that only the controller can prevent: validating a remote command against its own limits and recent local actions, assigning identifiers that are unique per record, acting on its own state where the hub's lags, failing a command it cannot deliver to the pump, and, for plans, the checks it makes at each occurrence. No hub rule replaces any of them.

    2. Before you start: credentials and roles¶

    Your controller is a device. It holds a native per-tenant credential, which belongs to a subject, and never signs in through the caregivers' identity service (AU1). Everything the hub binds to you is bound to that subject, never to a token, so rotating the token changes nothing (AU4).

    you need for who grants it rule case
    a credential whose subject is your controller's every request below; the binding of your product and instances the tenant: a native per-tenant credential for a device AU1, AU4, authority §3 accepted.yaml, update-token-rotated.yaml
    api:registrations:create creating and updating the product, adding definitions, creating and renewing instances included in the role a hub issues by default to a device or uploader credential (API_SECRET on single); the tenant owner may withhold it on the owner's listener authority §5, registration §7 not-authorised.yaml, instances/instance-create.yaml
    api:registrations:delete retiring the product, deleting an instance as api:registrations:create authority §5, registration §5 retire.yaml, retire-not-authorised.yaml
    to be the product's bound subject updating or retiring the product, adding a definition, holding a device command-sink the first create binds it; the owner can release or transfer it authority §3, authority §6 update-not-registration-owner.yaml, instances/instance-command-sink-not-registration-owner.yaml
    no command permission fetching your own commands and reporting their outcomes: the hub checks that you are the destination's bound subject — CM7, CM18 commands/fetch-naming-another-instance.yaml, commands/report-from-other-subject-refused.yaml
    (senders) api:commands:create; (followers) api:commands:read sending a command; reading a channel's outstanding list the tenant owner, to caregivers and follower apps CM5, CM24 commands/command-without-permission.yaml, commands/outstanding-lists-open-items.yaml

    Under TENANCY_MODE=single there is one listener and API_SECRET may register; the product is then bound to {kind: api-secret}, and any holder of the secret may update it (authority §3). Every holder of API_SECRET is one subject, so another app holding it can fetch your commands or report their outcomes; commands §9 lists this as open (UCA-CMD-014). Give the controller a credential of its own.

    Under TENANCY_MODE=multi each tenant has its own credential root and signing key (AU3). A registration is per tenant, so a controller that serves two tenants registers twice. A fetch-now signal is sent only inside a request bound to the command's tenant (CM19), and carries nothing (CM27); fetch is the path that always works.

    The parts of a hub your app calls (C4 containers; under TENANCY_MODE=single one listener serves all three, and API_SECRET acts as the owner):

    3.3 Create an instance, hold and hand off its lease¶

    An instance is one running copy of your controller: the phone that doses, a development build, a simulator, the old and the new phone during a migration (registration §8). It names a stored definition, claims roles on channels and renews a lease. TestController's override slot is the shared slot override, so it contends with any other product's instance on that slot; its extension's channel is its own, Example/TestController/extensions/com.example.testcontroller.adaptiveTargets.

    3.5 Receive a command, check it, enact it once, report the outcome¶

    A command names a channel. The hub sends it to the live device instance holding command-sink on that channel, on the command's tenant, and fixes that destination when it accepts the command (commands §2, CM2). A sender's command, as a case writes it, carries a payload and an expiry:

    at: 10
kind: command
tenant: A
subject: s-care
channel: override
product: ControllerA
payload:
  action: start
  preset: example
expiresAt: 600

    The capability document's commands member says whether the hub offers the command record, and where (CM31). The paths are proposed in commands §7.1 (CM32): you fetch at GET /api/v4/instances/{instanceId}/commands and report at POST /api/v4/commands/{commandId}/reports. The steps name the operation; the cases model calls, not requests.

    step you do the hub answers rule case

    2 Read the capability document's commands member at launch and after a release; without it the hub offers no command path, so keep your own transports with no member, every command path is 404 CM31 commands/discover-without-commands-capability.yaml, commands/discover-with-commands-capability.yaml
    3 Fetch with your own credential, naming your own instance, at least once per cycle or within the fetchIntervalSeconds you declare; a fetch returns each command, and each cancel carried to you, until you report, so a lost reply costs one fetch 200 with your commands only, again until you report; an empty list once you no longer hold the role; 403 not-registration-owner for another instance; the sender sees your bound as fetchWithinSeconds CM18, CM26, CM29, CM30 commands/fetch-returns-own-commands.yaml, commands/fetch-reply-lost-refetch-delivers.yaml, commands/refetch-after-acknowledged-empty.yaml, commands/fetch-after-role-lost-is-empty.yaml, commands/fetch-bound-default-cycle.yaml

    5. Checklist¶

    • The capability document is read at launch and after each release; with none, no submit, an unlisted grammar major or no instances, the controller writes carriers.
    • Every element in the registration is typed where it must be, versioned where its meaning can change, and uses {value: other, controllerTerm} for terms outside the shared lists.
    • Each kind that takes commands declares acceptsFromHub with command, and commands[] lists how commands reach the controller.
    • Each running copy has its own random UUID v4, a provenance on every role, labels without names or serials, and renews its lease before it lapses; a 412 at renewal is followed by a read.
    • A refused role or element sends that channel's or element's records as carriers; a load refusal is retried, never written as a carrier.
    • Every record has an identifier unique to it and stable across retries.
    • Fetch is implemented, at least once per cycle or within the declared fetchIntervalSeconds; a fetch-now signal is answered with a fetch and carries nothing.
    • Before enacting: the hash, the expiry by the controller's own clock, its limits and acceptance preference, and the doses and carbs since the command was made.
    • Each commandId is decided once, recorded across restarts until its expiry, and reported once with one final state and a reason from the shared list or in the other form.
    • A cancel carried by a fetch refuses a command not yet decided; a command already decided keeps its outcome.
