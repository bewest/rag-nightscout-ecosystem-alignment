    L5 Authority: who may register, write, own a site, receive a command, and act for someone else¶

    Living document, last changed 2026-10-18 at 85c645c8b.

    Contributor-facing. Living specification, current at main 1b69c295 (2026-10-18). Layer L5 of the platform index; terms are in the glossary. Decisions: D7 and D13–D17 (the admin plane, the credential root, the three listeners, the auth plane by audience), D18 and D19 and the maintainer's decisions of 2026-10-14 to 2026-10-18 recorded with them. Queue: SPEC-AUTHORITY.

    Specification 7 of 7 in the reading order; last, because it authorises what the others define. Expect rules AU1–AU5, binding and credential kinds, the owner's actions, namespaces, command destinations, delegation requirements DR1–DR7, what is recorded for audit, and the conformance cases.

    Who may register a controller's definitions, write under them, act as a site's owner, receive a command, and act for someone else. The hub routes and records; it never decides whether a change to therapy is safe, and it never doses (D18). Status: §2–§6 are decided or specified; §7 states delegation's requirements, and its design is open.

    2. Principals and credentials¶

    # rule status
    AU1 By audience. Devices and uploaders hold a native per-tenant credential permanently and never authenticate through Ory, because an uploader cannot run an OAuth flow. Tenant owners and caregivers are to authenticate through one cohort-wide Ory Kratos pool on hosted sites, with Nightscout minting a per-tenant token from the session. Third-party apps get no delegated-access server (Hydra deferred). The platform operator's plane needs no credential: it is unreachable from tenants (D7). D17; the Kratos row is adopted in principle and gated: no real identities until four production conditions hold at the edge and the compliance question has an owner
    AU2 Three listeners. ns-api and ns-realtime (and single) serve devices, clients and viewers; ns-tenant-admin serves the tenant owner, as a second bound socket inside ns-api; ns-admin serves the operator. Under single there is one listener, and the owner's actions are taken there with API_SECRET. An owner action on the data listener with any other credential is refused 403 not-authorised. D16; the socket decided 2026-10-16; API_SECRET as owner on single decided 2026-10-14
    AU3 The credential root is per tenant. Under TENANCY_MODE=multi there is no deployment-wide secret: each tenant holds its own root credential, tokens are signed with the tenant's own key, and tenant resolution runs before any credential is checked, so a signed token for one tenant fails the signature on another. That alone does not close an opaque token sent in a request body, which carries no signature; the subject store is also scoped under RLS (BF-25). Subject tokens are re-rooted on the tenant's credential. Credentials are per tenant, identity is per cohort, and authorization is per tenant, so isolation rests on the authorization layer and RLS, not on authentication. Under single, API_SECRET remains the bootstrap credential. D13 and its amendment, D14, D15; decided, not on dev
    AU4 Subjects, not tokens. A registration or an instance is bound to the subject that created it, never to a token, so a token rotated on the same subject changes nothing that is bound to it. Whether a delegation grant names subjects the same way is part of §7. decided 2026-10-14 (registration question 4; hub-sync §3.5 at b3536bbf)
    AU5 Permissions today. Writes are per collection (api:<collection>:<action>); reading is one bit: readable is *:*:read, every collection, every field, all time. shipping (cgm-remote-monitor lib/authorization/storage.js:235 at dev fade2299, read)

    3. Binding and credential kind¶

    A create or update binds the registration to the credential that wrote it: boundTo: {kind: subject, subject}, never the token, so a token rotated on the same subject changes nothing; or {kind: api-secret} for the shared secret on single, which any holder of the secret presents. The stored registration records credentialKind (subject, api-secret, or other with the hub's term), the kind that last created or updated it; both are returned with it. API_SECRET against a subject-bound registration is not-registration-owner on the data path. A released registration (boundTo: null) is bound by the next credential with the permission that updates it, even with identical content (200, not a no-op); it records bindingEvent: next-writer, kept by later updates under the same binding and cleared by a release or transfer, and not in the etag. When a single-tenant site moves into a tenant, a registration bound to api-secret is bound to the tenant's root credential, a further credential kind; the etag changes with boundTo (decided; the mechanics are SPEC-REG-MODES and SPEC-REG-MIGRATE).

    An instance is bound the same way, to the subject that created it, not to its product's binding, so a development build with its own subject may run a copy of a registered product (registration §8).

    4. The owner's actions¶

    On the owner's listener: ns-tenant-admin, or the one listener with API_SECRET on single. The owner never edits a registration's content (an owner PUT is 403 not-authorised) and never submits one. An action other than retire answers outcome applied with the resource after it; a request for what is already the case is a no-op.

    action effect
    retire registration §5.5
    block state blocked, recording the interrupted state as blockedFrom; etag changes; declaration, binding and stored data kept frozen; the controller's writes and typed writes are refused 403 blocked-by-owner. With If-None-Match: * it blocks an identity with nothing stored (stored with no registration), and a create for it is 403
    unblock restores the interrupted state (active, or retired); removes a pre-emptive block; never reactivates a retired registration. A blocked registration is unblocked before it is retired (409 blocked-by-owner otherwise)
    release boundTo: null; §3; drops non-matching device command-sinks (§6)
    transfer binds to a named subject; drops non-matching device command-sinks (§6)
    free-namespaces a retired registration's namespaces are no longer held by it (409 not-retired otherwise); its declaration and stored data stay readable
    transfer-role moves one role from its holder to a live instance that claims it with device provenance (409 role-not-granted, role-not-claimed or not-live otherwise); If-Match the etag of the instance it is moved from; both instances' etags change; the moved role records grantedBy: transfer-role
    pin-version, unpin-version registration §12
    listing every instance with its roles, labels and liveness; the overlaps; registrations (every stored product with its boundTo, credentialKind and bindingEvent); slotNameObservations; observed aliases only when asked

    An owner action on the data listener is 403 not-authorised, except with API_SECRET on single.

    5. Authorisation of registrations¶

    • The submitter is the controller, a device. It holds the native per-tenant credential (AU1). Submit, update, read and retire are on the data listener with that credential. Under TENANCY_MODE=multi the credential's token is signed with the tenant's key (AU3), so a registration is per tenant and a controller on two tenants registers twice. On single, API_SECRET may also register and is recorded as api-secret.
    • Permissions. Submitting needs api:registrations:create and retiring api:registrations:delete, granted by the tenant owner on ns-tenant-admin. Updating and retiring also need the binding. The names are proposals.
    • Storage. Registrations, definitions and instances are tenant data: per tenant, under RLS under multi (D3), never in the public capability document.

    Namespaces are first to claim, per tenant. A credential with no stated namespace roots may claim any namespace no other registration on the tenant holds. A hub may restrict a credential to owner-granted roots; a registration may then declare a namespace equal to or below one of them (com.example covers com.example.negotiation.targets; com.examplefoo does not), else namespace-not-owned. Either way a namespace another registration holds is namespace-claimed. A retired registration holds its namespaces until the owner frees them. Readers spanning tenants interpret a field by the declaration carried with it, never by its namespace. A published list of namespace conventions may record use; it is never a check.

    6. Who may receive a command¶

    A device command-sink role goes only to an instance whose binding equals the product's boundTo, or to one the owner moved it to with transfer-role (stored with grantedBy: transfer-role); any other device claim is refused not-registration-owner for that role, after the channel checks and before exclusivity. A simulated command-sink claim is open to any permitted subject and never receives a routed command. Source roles are open to any permitted subject. When a product's boundTo changes (the owner's transfer or release, or a released registration bound by its next writer), every device command-sink role held by an instance whose binding no longer matches is dropped at once and stored refused not-registration-owner, and that instance's etag changes; roles with grantedBy: transfer-role are kept. A held device command-sink survives renewal only when the instance matches the binding or the role carries grantedBy.

    Where a command goes once a holder exists, and that the hub never routes to a guess, is commands §2. The command record and the proposal in front of a command are proposed in commands §4–§7.

    7. Delegation (not decided)¶

    Delegation lets a principal give a caregiver, an app or an agent a part of their authority. Status: requirements stated (DR1–DR7); design open; not implemented.

    What is decided around it:

    • Validation and enactment stay the controller's; the hub routes and records and does not judge whether a change is safe (D18).
    • An agent that starts an intent is recorded as an initiator in the open form {value: other, controllerTerm}; no agent value is added to any vocabulary (maintainer, 2026-10-18; state and intent S8).
    • A role for an agent, such as proposing or acting, is an other role a hub lists (registration §9).
    • The proposal record in front of a command belongs to the commands specification (maintainer, 2026-10-18).
    • Third-party delegated access through an authorization server is deferred (AU1).

    What a delegation design would have to provide, stated 2026-09-10 as requirements for qualified review (configuration and delegation §5.4):

    # requirement today
    DR1 scoped reads, enforced in storage, so a forgotten filter returns nothing reading is one bit (AU5)
    DR2 bounded queries, because a read grant is otherwise a denial-of-service grant unbounded on 15.0.8: API v1 passes query operators through; an operator allowlist is merged on dev, not released; an unbounded pattern-matching cost defect (BF-72) is open on 15.0.8 and dev. D8 sets API v3's closed operator set as the query contract, which bounds the operators, not their cost
    DR3 purpose and expiry as fields of the grant, not documentation modelled for writes in the authority model; not implemented
    DR4 answers rather than data where they suffice, a smaller disclosure than raw reads nothing
    DR5 an audit of what was read writes are timed (srvModified); reads are not recorded
    DR6 revocation that is real, which argues for narrow, short-lived grants, since a read cannot be un-read nothing
    DR7 minimization at the boundary, as this repository's de-identification tools do the tools exist for research exports only

    Proposed constraints, carried from agents in the ecosystem §4.3 and specified as proposed rules in commands §6: read access never confers a propose or act role, so a readable credential, or a tool holding one, cannot be promoted to either (CM12); an approval binds to the exact change, by the SHA-256 of its canonical payload (CM13); and the state a proposal was based on is the hub's state version (state and intent A12), checked by the hub at approval (CM15). That version is the hub's record of what the controller reported, so it can tell an approver that the record changed, never that the controller's own state is unchanged.

    Prior art. The upstream agent control-plane RFC's agent issuer type, its suggest scope ("can propose, human must approve") and its DelegationGrant (who granted, to whom, scopes, constraints, expiry, revocation); the authority model; and nightscout-roles-gateway's time-bounded policies. The RFC's rule that composes conflicting intents inside the hub is outside the hub's role (D18).

    8. What is recorded for audit¶

    • Current state, not history, on the owner's listing (§4): each product's binding, the kind of credential that last wrote it and how a released product was last bound; each live instance's roles, provenance and overlaps. A release or transfer clears the binding event, and a deleted instance leaves the listing.
    • Attribution of typed records: each carries its instance, definition and the role's provenance (registration §11). The stable link to who wrote it is the instance's own binding to the subject that created it (§3); the product's binding is today's, and can change after the write. A namespace string proves nothing about authorship.
    • Who started an intent, in the controller's vocabulary (state and intent S8).
    • Not recorded: who took an owner action and when; reads (DR5); and, on today's collections, who wrote a record beyond the free-text enteredBy, an identifying field that is not an identity.

    9. Open¶

    1. Choices for the maintainer to confirm: unblock restores the interrupted state; a blocked registration is unblocked before it is retired; credentialKind is the kind that last created or updated a registration; reactivation reclaims a freed namespace only if no other registration holds it.
    2. D17 row 2: caregivers and owners on Ory Kratos, adopted in principle and gated.
    3. The contract per tenancy mode (SPEC-REG-MODES) and across a site's move into a tenant (SPEC-REG-MIGRATE).
    4. Owner actions with no case: an owner PUT (403), retiring a blocked registration before unblocking it (409), and transfer-role refused role-not-granted or not-live.
    5. A record of owner actions (who and when), which §8 says is not kept.
    6. Delegation (§7): the grant's shape, where a grant is stored and checked, read scopes, read audit, purpose limitation and revocation, and whether an agent instance is scoped by label, by role or by its own subject.
