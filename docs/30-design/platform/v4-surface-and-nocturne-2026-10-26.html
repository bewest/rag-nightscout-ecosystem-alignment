    API v4: which parts to share with Nocturne, and which listener serves each¶

    Record

    Record as of 2026-10-26. A point-in-time document; it is not kept up to date.

    Contributor-facing. Snapshot, 2026-10-26: alignment main 6e6223f26 (specifications; amended the same day for the maintainer's decisions in §0), cgm-remote-monitor draft PR #8857 at 3be98536, Nocturne main 89dcaf1dd. Every Nocturne statement below was read from source at 89dcaf1dd and nothing was run against it (read-derived). The proposed surface is described in specs/openapi/proposed/nightscout-v4.yaml; the current API in specs/openapi/upstream/cgm-remote-monitor/. Nocturne paths are relative to src/API/Nocturne.API/ unless they begin with Core. (under src/Core/Nocturne.) or tests/. Nothing here has been sent to either project.

    The maintainer asked, on 2026-10-26, three things: how much of API v4 to unify with Nocturne, how much is split by scope between the operator's and owner's listeners and the app listener, and whether controller registration and instances belong on v4 endpoints. The maintainer's direction the same day: Nocturne's v4 surface is to be split, and parts of it will sit on different listeners. This page therefore treats Nocturne's surface as material to partition, group by group, and states for each group which listener it belongs on in our specifications and whether it is a candidate for a shared contract.

    0. Decided, 2026-10-26¶

    The maintainer decided four points the same day; the sections below are amended for them.

    point decision where it is applied
    statistics served on v4 under statistics (/api/v4/statistics/...), matching Nocturne 89dcaf1dd's StatisticsController for hub compatibility: its paths, parameters, response shapes, tenant time-zone bucketing, the under-ten-readings rule and its read scopes. The design stays open for discussion: what a shared contract fixes and what it leaves to each hub, and whether the compute-over-a-posted-body actions join later operations 32–41; specs/openapi/proposed/statistics-v4.schema.json; §6.4; dated notes on the two older texts
    registration scope registration of products, definitions and instances is a per-tenant API that affects only that tenant's site and data, never a platform or operator action; the owner's listing and actions are tenant-scoped (tenant-admin under multi, the one listener under single); controller actions and commands are on the data listener authority AU6; registration's opening note
    instance paths one flat root: /api/v4/instances/{instanceId} is the instance resource, its command fetch /api/v4/instances/{instanceId}/commands, and the owner's listing GET /api/v4/instances; creating an instance names the product in the body registration §7 amendment; commands §7.1; the instance schema's product; operations 8–10; §5.1
    softwareId proposed in the registration's metadata, in Nocturne's RFC 7591 software_id form, for compatibility; its rules stay open for discussion controller-state-model.schema.json; registration §5.1; §5

    1. The answer in brief¶

    • Registration and instances on v4: yes, already. The specifications put the product, definition and instance resources under the capability document's submit.href, and #8857 serves it at /api/v4/registrations (lib/registration/index.js:25); the command paths of commands §7.1 are under /api/v4/ too, and instances have their own flat root (§0). Registration is per tenant (AU6). Discovery stays at /.well-known/nightscout/ (RFC 8615). What is not settled is the path the owner's actions take on ns-tenant-admin (§7, decisions 1–2).
    • Split by listener. Of the 41 proposed operations, 30 are on the data listener, 10 on the tenant-owner listener, and one (retire) on both, by caller. None is on the operator listener: the operator's plane (tenant lifecycle, export) is not a v4 surface in our design (D7). Nocturne serves all three audiences from one API and divides them by scope and role (§4).
    • What to propose to Nocturne first: discovery, the registration grammar with its product, definition and instance resources, the command and proposal paths, and statistics reads, all on the data listener. The owner's actions are shared as rules, not as a listener: each hub keeps its own way of refusing them to device and app credentials. Typed record and state-span paths come second, after the identity decisions. Everything else in Nocturne's v4 stays hub-specific (§6).

    4. How each side divides the audiences¶

    audience our specifications Nocturne at 89dcaf1dd
    devices, uploaders, apps the data listener with a native per-tenant credential (AU1); permissions are shiro strings, api:<collection>:<action>, and reading is one bit (AU5); the new names api:registrations:*, api:commands:*, api:proposals:* are proposals the same API; OAuth clients registered by RFC 7591 hold category scopes in two tiers, glucose.read / glucose.readwrite and so on (Core.Models/Authorization/Scope.cs:31-133)
    the tenant owner its own listener, ns-tenant-admin; an owner action on the data listener with any other credential is 403 not-authorised (AU2); on single, API_SECRET the same API; the tenant-administration atoms (roles.manage, members.invite, members.manage, tenant.settings, sharing.manage, audit.read, audit.manage, Scope.cs:93-106) are grantable to a member's role and are not requestable by an OAuth client: "there is no per-client scope ceiling to bound a request for them and no consent screen that could meaningfully describe them" (Scope.cs:17-21, sets at :140, :171, :182). Maintenance controllers require the full-access scope * (Attributes/PermissionAttributes.cs:96)
    the operator ns-admin, its own process and port, no credential, unreachable from tenants (D7, after ORY's public and admin ports) the same API under /api/v4/admin/* with [Authorize(Roles = "platform_admin")]; the role is taken only from a credential that "is the person" (session, OIDC token, legacy JWT, platform-access token), never from a grant, API secret or OAuth token (Core.Models/Authorization/PlatformAdminCredentials.cs:10-45; Middleware/AuthenticationMiddleware.cs:114-131, :151-154). Server-to-server cross-tenant calls use X-Instance-Key only (Attributes/RequireInstanceKeyAuthAttribute.cs:6-36)
    an anonymous viewer none on v4 public share links hold reports.read, so statistics compute calls are reachable without an account (Extensions/ServiceRegistrationExtensions.cs:457-461)

    Where they agree. Both refuse owner powers to a credential issued to a device or an app. Ours does it by the listener the request arrives on (AU2); Nocturne's by keeping the owner atoms out of the set an OAuth client may request (Scope.cs:17-21) and the operator role out of every delegated credential (PlatformAdminCredentials.cs). Both keep the operator's powers out of every tenant credential.

    Where they differ. Ours separates audiences by socket; Nocturne by scope and role on one API. A search of src/API for Listen*, RequireHost and MapWhen finds none; Program.cs:81 configures Kestrel only for the request body limit. Our operator plane has no credential at all; Nocturne's operator is a person holding platform_admin. Our registration permissions are collection-style strings; Nocturne's scopes are per data category, which is closer to the scoped reads authority DR1 asks for.

    The consequence for a shared contract. A rule written as "on ns-tenant-admin" cannot be met by a hub with one listener. A rule written as "an owner action is refused to any credential a device or app holds, with 403 not-authorised" is met by both: by AU2's listener, and by Nocturne's atom partition. The conformance cases owner-action-on-data-listener and instances/owner-transfer-role-data-listener already state the refusal; their listener member is how a hub with listeners runs them.

What each controller's documents mean is compiled into the server.

    Whether they compose. They answer different questions and do not overlap: Nocturne's registration says who is writing and with what scopes; ours says what the writes mean and which copy wrote them. PROPOSAL-controller-descriptions §2.2 proposes joining them on the reverse-DNS software_id. On 2026-10-26 the maintainer decided to propose it in Nocturne's form for compatibility: metadata.softwareId is now in controller-state-model.schema.json, marked proposed, optional, compared exactly as Nocturne's directory lookup compares it (KnownOAuthClients.cs:214-215), and not part of the registration's identity (registration §5.1). Nocturne's registration request carries it as software_id with no format check (Models/OAuth/ClientRegistrationRequest.cs:41-44). Composed, a Nocturne client registered as org.loopkit.loop would submit a registration whose metadata names the same id, and a hub could read both halves from one key. On our side the binding is to the subject (AU4), which under Nocturne's model would be the OAuth client's subject. Open for discussion: whether two products may share one softwareId, and whether a hub checks it against the credential's registered client.

    5.1 Are registrations and instances redundant?¶

    The maintainer asked whether registrations and instances are redundant, or whether one is for the kind and the other for the running copy, and if redundant whether one should go. They are not redundant (registration §7–§8, D19):

    resource says identity lifetime
    product registration, with its immutable definitions what a controller's data means: the kind, per vendor and product, per release (vendor, product); each definition by the hash of its content as long as the controller publishes it; definitions are never changed
    instance which running copy is connected: its definition, its roles on channels (source of record, command sink), its labels a random UUID v4 the copy generates once a lease the copy renews; a lapsed instance holds no roles

    Several instances run one definition (a phone and a lab rig, or the old and the new phone during a migration), and one instance is the destination of commands for a channel. Removing either loses something: without the product there is nothing to say what the writes mean; without instances there is no way to tell two copies apart or to route a command to one.

    The wrinkle was two path roots for one instance. Options considered:

    option form evidence
    (a) keep both roots /api/v4/registrations/{vendor}/{product}/instances/{id}, fetch at /api/v4/instances/{id}/commands commands.fetch is a URI template in the capability document, so clients do not hard-code it; #8857 serves the nested form
    (b) nest the fetch under the product …/{vendor}/{product}/instances/{id}/commands every fetch would carry the vendor and product, which the fetch rules (CM18, CM26) never use
    (c) one flat root /api/v4/instances/{id} and /api/v4/instances/{id}/commands an instance id is a random UUID v4 (identity §2), so it names one copy without its product; the copy keeps one path for its life

    Decided, maintainer 2026-10-26: (c). Creating an instance names the product in the body (product: {vendor, product} in controller-instance.schema.json), not by a create on the product resource that answers with a flat Location. The evidence for the body: the instance id is chosen by the controller and the create is a PUT to it (registration §8), so create, renew and release use one path with no redirect; the order of checks already reads the product's registration, binding, block and retirement for every PUT, and the stored instance and the owner's listing already carry vendor and product (registration-submit.schema.json storedInstance.identity, instanceSummary); and the conformance cases model an instance request with its identity separately from the path, so none changes. A renewal naming another product is 422 invalid-instance. Nocturne has no instance resource; the nearest, its client devices, is flat: /api/v4/client-devices/{id} (ClientDevicesController.cs:21, :127, :150).

    6.2 The minimum shared v4 contract¶

    1. Discovery: GET /.well-known/nightscout/sync and /controllers, with the capability document's schema.
    2. Registration: the grammar (controller-state-model.schema.json), the product, definition and instance resources with their etag and precondition rules, the element results, and the fallback rule; the owner's actions as rules with the refusal stated per credential (§4).
    3. Commands: the command record, the outstanding list, fetch and report, cancel, and the proposal in front of a command.
    4. Statistics: Nocturne's ten reads over stored data, with its time-zone and minimum-data rules and read scopes (decided, §6.4).

    Second wave, after the decisions they depend on: typed record paths (G3) and state spans (G4).

    6.3 Credit¶

    The record catalogue is Nocturne's V4 model (records §3). The partition of scopes into what an OAuth client may request and what a tenant role may confer (Scope.cs:17-21) is the closest prior art for stating the owner boundary as a rule on credentials rather than on sockets; §4 proposes exactly that. The reverse-DNS software_id directory is Nocturne's. The leases, labels and selectors, the declared-versus-observed split and the resource-with-etag style are taken from Kubernetes (PROPOSAL-controller-descriptions §4); the operator plane secured by unreachability is ORY's public and admin port split (D7).
