    Durable event delivery contract for multitenant consumers¶

    Record

    Record as of 2026-11-06. A point-in-time document; it is not kept up to date.

    Contributor-facing design contract, 2026-11-06, design direction adopted by the maintainer. Batch B7 of the integration batching plan; queue home MT-RUNTIME-PLAN (in-progress, no runnable gate yet). It reconciles D-EVENT's in-process commit event with D6's change-feed design and the transactional tenant clock, and names the entry bar before any distributed evaluator or realtime wiring. It promotes no milestone, selects no provider, admits nothing to a release candidate, and infers no consistency stronger than what the cited records reproduced.

    0. Premise, as read¶

    • D-EVENT (maintainer, 2026-10-31; decisions.md D-EVENT question 1 row) names one in-process event per write(intent), carrying the step's items[]; delivery between processes is deferred to the multitenant roles; a timer serves time-driven alarms only, never catch-up on writes; writers that bypass Nightscout are unsupported. Read-derived.
    • The event as built at 35903069 is storage-intent-committed (lib/storage/write-intent.js:4635), documented at :4622-4634 as "INTERNAL: nothing in lib/ listens". Read-derived.
    • D6 (execution plan §1, row D6): change feed = replication slot (spine) + NOTIFY (latency) + bounded aggregate poll (backstop), NOTIFY emitted by the slot reader, never by a trigger. T4.1 (same plan, Phase 4) reproduced 200/200 rows delivered after a stopped reader returned, 0 on re-consume, and a 0.000000% notification-queue gauge with reader-emitted NOTIFY against 0.019073% from a trigger.
    • The roadmap §4 names this reconciliation as a design task, and its MT-RUNTIME-PLAN packet asks for durable checkpoint ownership, commit coupling, ordering, duplicates, hard and soft deletes, configuration invalidation and restart boundaries, with executable controls, before one producer/consumer recovery slice.
    • The seams plan "Shared frame join and multitenant recovery" says a local EventEmitter and a cell lease cannot establish these guarantees, and that provider selection stays open.

    The event payload at the anchored source carries no tenant or stamp. B2 has since joined the clock locally; the R56 report owns that measured baseline. The original source references below remain anchored to the table above; they are not line references into the joined source.

    1. Terms¶

    Term Meaning in this contract
    logical intent event the record of one resolved write(intent): collection, source, op, per-record _id, identifier, outcome and problems, counts, collapsed flag (write-intent.js:4656-4678). It names records; it carries no document contents and no filter (D-EVENT question 3)
    commit stamp the per-tenant monotone value the transactional tenant clock allocates inside the data transaction and holds through COMMIT; persisted on the committed rows as the server-owned modification field. The ordering fence of the clock proof plan
    checkpoint one consumer's durable position for one tenant: the highest stamp whose complete equal-stamp cohort that consumer has processed. A database row owned by the consumer, never process memory
    consumer a process that derives state from committed writes: the evaluator role (plugins, notifications), the realtime role (socket fan-out), a cache, a view materializer. Each has its own checkpoint per tenant
    tenant-local evaluator the consumer that runs the plugin and notification cycle for one tenant from that tenant's rows under its own tenant binding, retaining one cycle per tenant (R50's policy)
    backstop the bounded aggregate poll of D6: a periodic per-tenant read of rows with stamp above the checkpoint, which finds what a lost NOTIFY did not announce
    timer the time-driven trigger (bootevent.js:526 tick today) that evaluates alarms whose condition is elapsed time, not a new row

    2. The spine: what is durable and what is not¶

    Thing Durable Basis
    the committed rows of a tenant, with their server-owned dates and (under the clock) their commit stamp yes: this is the spine lower-port proof: rollback leaves "data and durable floor unchanged" and "no queued logical event escapes" (reproduced, tenant-commit-clock-lower-proof)
    the tenant clock floor row yes: readiness refuses a missing or stale floor before binding (reproduced, lower-port and HTTP fixed-recovery reports)
    the in-process storage-intent-committed event no: built and emitted only while a listener is attached (write-intent.js:4682); an observer failure is logged and the write stays committed (:4686); nothing in lib/ listens read-derived
    the PostgreSQL ports' post-commit emission no: withCommitEvents emits after connection.transaction() resolves (postgres-intent-common.js:42-48), which has verified COMMIT (postgres-connection.js:59-62); a crash between those two lines loses the emission and keeps the rows read-derived; "a failed mutating transaction publishes nothing" is reproduced by the port corpus (lib/storage/PORT-CONTRACT.md:141-146)
    the settle emission for a resolved no-op no, and it is not a spine event at all: it opens no transaction and proves no physical write (PORT-CONTRACT.md:120-124, postgres-intent-common.js:49-53) read-derived
    NOTIFY no: latency only (D6); a wedged consumer does not fill the queue when the reader emits it (T4.1, reproduced 2026-09-15)
    a replication slot's unacknowledged WAL durable while the slot exists; retention horizon is deferred (§9) T4.1 pinned 234.5 KB for 200 rows across a stop (reproduced)

    Rules.

    1. The spine is the committed rows plus their stamp. A consumer that has the rows and its checkpoint needs no event to be correct; events only make it faster. This is the D6 shape and the D-EVENT scope decision read together.
    2. A consumer's position is a per-tenant checkpoint stamp owned by the consumer and stored in the database, under that tenant's binding, never in process memory. Two consumers never share a checkpoint row; the evaluator and realtime roles advance independently.
    3. The logical intent event must carry the tenant and the commit stamp of the write it describes before it is an interprocess message. At 35903069 it carries neither (write-intent.js:4676-4677); D-EVENT question 3 already reserved "later the tenant". A consumer that receives an event without a stamp treats it as a wake-up only and reads from its checkpoint. Current joined implementation: after-COMMIT callbacks receive the final transaction floor; stamp is null for unclocked, no-allocation and settle events. R71 owns the physical component proof and R72 owns comprehensive integration and the explicit nullable single-site settle assertions. The following initial allocation-time prototype is historical. Built on lane codex/event-payload-20261106 (head 4b5edf02, three commits over 65bb8456, unpushed): withCommitEvents.write captures the tenant from the bound scope and the stamp from a new connection.currentStamp() on the clock decorator when the nested write resolves; settle, Mongo and clock-off carry null. Unit 9 passing, controls red for a null stamp (7 / 2) and a remembered tenant (6 / 3), focused clock run 28 / 1 / 0; the Mongo-gated case is UNMEASURED. The figures are the lane's own (HANDOFF-event-payload-20261106.md), not re-run here.
    4. Commit coupling: the stamp is allocated inside the data transaction and held to COMMIT (clock proof plan "Proposed invariant"; postgres-write-clock.js:52 per the HTTP report, read-derived at the clock head); publication happens strictly after the transaction resolves, never inside it, and never from a database trigger (D6).

    3. Ordering and duplicates¶

    Delivery is at-least-once. Every consumer is idempotent on (tenant, stamp, collection, identity), where identity is the stored _id with its typed form kept distinct from a same-spelling string (write-intent.js:4640-4642 keys them apart; the port corpus preserves typed forms in delete records, PORT-CONTRACT.md:125).

    Guarantee Holds Basis
    Two writes for one tenant that both commit receive distinct, increasing stamps, and an exclusive cursor taken after the higher one commits includes the lower one yes, under the clock, for the schedules run: lock wait observed while the lower write is held, no early visibility, ordered rows after release reproduced, lower-port "transaction clock fence"; HTTP "same-tenant concurrent writers" (1793991708168 → 1793991708169)
    The same, without the clock no: an authored detached counter commits distinct stamps yet the lower, later-committing row becomes invisible behind an already-observed higher exclusive cursor reproduced as the alternative-design control, lower-port report
    Parallel REST reads of two collections share one database revision no reproduced, R49 "parallel reads do not provide a common database revision"
    Every row has a unique stamp no: two identifier-absent typed twins marked by one mutation share a stamp; a sorted LIMIT 1 read followed by an exclusive cursor omits the tied second row reproduced, lower-port "existing bulk tie characterization" (that cohort and schedule, not every interface)
    Stamps across the two write paths (v3 identifier writes and legacy filter writes) are one sequence yes under the clock for the exercised plan: after an intervening committed v3 cursor, two distinct preplanned legacy targets receive later authoritative stamps reproduced, lower-port "earlier legacy plan"; raw import and mixed enabled/disabled writers are outside the captures
    Equal wall clocks in separate API workers give distinct stamps yes reproduced, HTTP report (1793991706986 → ...987 → ...988)

    Rules that follow.

    1. Equal-stamp cohorts are the unit of progress. A consumer records checkpoint s only after it has read every row with stamp s. A page cut by a limit inside a cohort records the previous stamp, not s. The cursor is stamp > checkpoint, ordered by stamp then identity, and the page size is larger than the largest bulk write the writer admits (the step's resolve cap, RESOLVE_CAP, bounds the named records in one event; the bulk soft-delete case is the reason this rule exists).
    2. Duplicates are expected, not errors. A replayed row with (tenant, stamp, collection, identity) at or below the checkpoint is a no-op. A replayed row above it is processed once.
    3. Ordering is per tenant only. No cross-tenant order is promised or needed; tenant B progresses while tenant A's writers wait on A's clock row (reproduced, lower-port "cross-tenant progress" on a shared pool of four; HTTP report, same observation).
    4. No cross-collection snapshot. A consumer that reads entries and treatments in two statements may see a later treatment with an earlier entries cohort (R49). The evaluator's answer is the retained-cycle policy (§7), not a snapshot; a stronger policy needs its own implementation and test (seams plan).
    5. Without the clock the contract is not satisfiable by cursor replay. On a backend or configuration without the commit-coupled stamp (single-tenant MongoDB under D4, which opens no transaction on a standalone server, seam interface §9.1; or PostgreSQL with the opt-in clock off), the in-process bus stays the mechanism and this contract does not apply. That is the D-EVENT scope decision, not a gap.

    4. Deletes¶

    Kind Status under the contract Basis
    soft delete (the v3 default and every path that marks a record invalid) an ordinary write: the row stays, its stamp advances, and a consumer that replays it removes the record from its view. A bulk soft delete is one event with many records (or collapsed above the cap) and one cohort that may share a stamp (§3 rule 1) read-derived from the step; the tie case reproduced in the lower-port report
    hard delete through the intent path (v3 prune/autoPrune, write-intent.js:4606-4613; permanent delete) outside the spine. The event names the removed ids, but no durable row carries a stamp afterwards, so replay cannot reconstruct it. Strict replay cannot recover absence. The adopted full current-state snapshot backstop below handles absence separately; tombstones remain deferred (§9) read-derived stamped-spine limit; separate native characterization and the adopted reconciliation direction are linked in §11
    raw writers that bypass write(intent) (a direct MongoDB upload, an independent SQL client) outside the contract, by decision: writers that go straight to the database are unsupported and xDrip+'s MongoDB upload is deprecated (D-EVENT question 1 row, decisions.md). R50's fixture used exactly such raw writes to show they "emit no process event" (reproduced); under the clock they also carry no stamp allocated by the fence

    A consumer never infers a hard delete from a missing event. Strict stamped replay retains the limits above. The later bounded snapshot reconciliation decision selects full admitted current-identity/absence reconciliation in a consistent tenant snapshot, independent of floor movement. An arbitrary recent-time window is not sufficient. It preserves existing hard deletes and unstamped activity behavior; explicit row/byte refusal is required. Cadence/capacity and implementation acceptance remain unmeasured; every-transition history remains separate.

    5. Configuration and grant invalidation¶

    Decision: re-read per cycle, not an event. Current authority is checked per operation from a tenant-bound snapshot taken for that operation, and the evaluator binds each cycle to the settings version it read at the start of the cycle.

    Why this and not an invalidation event:

    • R48 reproduced that the shipping data-access path consumes one tenant-bound SQL snapshot "for every permission and current-name attribution in the operation", with no retained cross-request permission cache; its controls show the failure mode of the alternative exactly: "reusing an earlier snapshot makes committed grant removal's next GET answer 200 instead of 403". Revocation-as-event would reintroduce that cache and make its correctness depend on at-least-once delivery, the property this contract exists to make tolerable rather than load-bearing.
    • A grant revocation and a settings change are per-tenant configuration rows under D15 and D22; they are not writes to a data collection and do not pass through write(intent), so they have no logical intent event to ride on today. Whether configuration rows also appear on the feed is deferred (§9); a consumer never relies on it.
    • R51 keyed its settings differential to actual settings rows and refused on stale state (reproduced, 12/0 with three semantic controls). Reading the settings version at cycle start costs one indexed read per cycle, the same order as the T4.1 payload decision ("a consumer told only 'tenant T changed' reads under its own tenant binding").

    Consequences: a revoked grant stops delivery to the affected socket at the next operation that checks authority, and the realtime role performs that check per fan-out cycle, not per connection lifetime (the execution plan already notes that a tenant suspended mid-session is not re-checked after the handshake under the current socket; that is the behaviour this rule replaces for the multitenant roles). A settings change takes effect at the next cycle; retained state keyed to the old version is discarded (§7).

    6. Restart and catch-up¶

    Situation What happens Covered by
    consumer starts, or restarts after a crash reads its checkpoint for each tenant it serves, replays stamp > checkpoint in cohort order, records the new checkpoint after each complete cohort, then subscribes to NOTIFY replay from the spine
    crash after COMMIT, before the emission (postgres-intent-common.js:46-47) the rows and stamp are durable; the emission is lost; replay finds the rows replay, never the publish
    crash inside the transaction nothing is visible, floor unchanged, no event escapes; the writer gets a sanitized 503 and the same worker continues reproduced: lower-port "actual rollback"; HTTP fixed-recovery operator-abort case (STORAGE_UNAVAILABLE, then GET 200 and POST 201 from the same worker)
    lost NOTIFY the bounded aggregate poll reads stamp > checkpoint on its interval and finds the cohort backstop (D6)
    slot reader stopped and restarted rows written meanwhile are delivered once on return; the slot is acknowledged only after the batch's notifications commit reproduced, T4.1 (200/200, 0 on re-consume), on the 2026-09-15 reader
    time passes with no writes (stale data, missed readings, pump age) the timer evaluates time-driven alarms from the retained cycle timer; it reads no new rows and advances no checkpoint
    a writer's process is killed mid-transaction while holding the fence the backend stays active until the lock holder is released; the survivor advances the floor by one; this is not a rapid-recovery bound reproduced characterization, HTTP report "Node SIGKILL" row; the bound is UNMEASURED

    The rule the roadmap states is kept verbatim in effect: the timer may drive time-based alarms; it never silently repairs lost write events. A consumer that finds its checkpoint missing starts from the floor the operator provisioned, and says so in its health report; it does not invent one.

    7. Evaluator and notification state¶

    R73 owns current functional M5 acceptance. The historical source inventory below remains anchored to the earlier source in §0; it is not a current code inventory.

    The later tenant-wide suppression decision settles first-preview ack/snooze effects: shared within the tenant for the alarm group/level, original absolute expiry retained through restart, and no extension from repeats during the active window. Preserve the existing default duration and level-2-to-level-1 effect. Per-user/device suppression is separate. Storage shape and historical retention are engineering/operational work; the unchanged client protocol supplies no durable request identity for indefinite post-expiry deduplication. R73 records bounded persistence, crash/retry and cross-tenant acceptance. Live provider/device delivery and operating/retention budgets remain separate.

    Contract for the multitenant roles, borrowing R50's retained-cycle policy and the seams plan's identity rule:

    1. Evaluator and notification state is keyed by (tenant, settings version, plugin set) with a bounded lifetime; a key change discards the retained cycle rather than carrying it across versions. There is no process-global mutable clinical frame.
    2. A failed clinical cycle retains the prior successful result and timestamp and creates no new ordinary/alarm notification request; the next real trigger recovers (R50, R55 frame-retention control: null replacing the kept properties is the red). Independently committed, authorized ACK/snooze controls under D23 remain effective and may produce a control-clear while acquisition fails. That control effect does not mark the clinical cycle successful.
    3. Ack and snooze are per tenant writes. D23 names their writer: the client acks on the /alarm socket as today, realtime forwards the ack, like every other socket write, unchanged to api's internal write endpoint, and api persists a tenant-keyed ack row that the evaluator reads from the feed like any other committed row, so an ack survives a consumer restart by replay and realtime holds no storage write authority. A forward that fails yields no acknowledgement, so the client's existing no-ack retry applies. The roadmap keeps "owner ack/snooze and actual notification delivery" on their separate gates, so the row's shape and historical retention remain implementation/operational work; the later decision above settles suppression scope, original expiry and active-window repeats.
    4. The retained cycle's inputs are the tenant's rows at or below the consumer's checkpoint; the evaluation instant is recorded with the cycle and is not a database revision (R49).

    Durable notification request handoff¶

    The maintainer adopted durable requests and retries for the explicit multitenant preview after reviewing actual-component/synthetic- provider ambiguity evidence on 57d54963. That evidence models lost process-local state through component recreation; it is not a measured process crash, live provider acceptance or device delivery. The decision selects the responsibility for retry; joined implementation acceptance remains separate.

    1. Persist tenant-bound accepted request intent and a stable logical request identity before dispatch. Retry that identity after restart or an ambiguous provider result. Input checkpoint/floor alone is not an identity: time-only evaluation and unstamped changes can produce distinct evaluated outputs.
    2. Persisted intent, send attempt, adapter acceptance/receipt and device delivery are distinct facts. A lost response after external acceptance can lead to a repeated notification. Clinical-record write identity prevents neither that ambiguity nor the repeated external effect. No exactly-once delivery is claimed.
    3. Preserve existing normal alarm/snooze/repeat-window behavior and tenant-wide suppression/original expiry. Current authority, configuration identity and suppression must govern dispatch/retry; a queued request cannot bypass them. Failed clinical cycles enqueue no new ordinary/alarm request. Independently committed, authorized ACK/snooze controls may enqueue a control-clear; their effects are distinct from clinical-cycle success. Shutdown fences late dispatch/publication.
    4. Preserve the single-tenant path and existing client/wire shapes. New durable preview recovery is not evidence of equivalent external failure behavior: retrying an uncertain send can repeat an alert. Reuse shared seams only with explicit default/single compatibility verification.
    5. Joined M5 acceptance exercises actual notification consumers with controlled adapters: ordinary alarm, snooze, active repeat window, restart, ambiguous acceptance, failed cycle, foreign tenant/configuration and shutdown. Verify stable retry identity and persisted state independently. Actual selected provider/device delivery retains its own gate. Uploader corpus passes cannot substitute for notification-consumer evidence. Select actual browser alarm presentation/ack consumers and existing notification cache contracts, with controlled Pushover/Maker-compatible sinks where admitted. Run joined restricted-PG/process-death, lost-completion and same-identity retry controls, including snooze/config changes before retry and D23 ack/no-ack stored outcomes. Pinned mobile socket-pattern replay is not execution of those mobile binaries; neither proves live provider/device delivery.

    Request storage/state-machine layout and a controlled synthetic sink are implementation choices within these effects. Provider selection, retry timing, queue retention and capacity need their own measurements/contracts; no numeric budget or live egress is authorized by this decision.

    8. Entry bar before any implementation¶

    The roadmap asks for executable controls before one producer/consumer recovery slice. Each row names the predicted green and the exact red symptom; a control that goes red for a setup reason is not evidence (clock proof plan). The rows below retain the original predicted controls. R73 owns the actual executed mechanisms, physical observations and criterion mapping; a predicted mutation is not evidence that that exact experiment ran.

    Control Predicted green assertion Exact red symptom when the mechanism is broken
    kill after commit, before publish, then restart (pause the consumer-facing emission after connection.transaction() resolves; kill the writer and the consumer; restart the consumer) the restarted consumer's tenant view equals a fresh read of the tenant's rows; its checkpoint equals the highest committed stamp break: checkpoint stored in process memory. Red: the view lacks the last committed cohort and the checkpoint reads the pre-kill value
    duplicate replay (deliver the same cohort twice, once by NOTIFY and once by the backstop) one evaluation, one notification request, same properties; checkpoint unchanged after the second delivery break: idempotency key dropped to (tenant, stamp) or to identity alone. Red: two notification requests for one write, or a typed twin collapses onto its string spelling
    soft delete replayed after restart the row is absent from the restarted consumer's view and from the realtime delta break: consumer skips records whose outcome is deleted. Red: the deleted record is still in the view
    equal-stamp cohort cut by a page with a page size smaller than one bulk soft delete, every record of the cohort is processed before the checkpoint advances break: record the page's last stamp. Red: the tied second row (the lower-port characterization) is never processed
    revoked grant (remove a grant while a socket is connected) delivery to that socket stops within one fan-out cycle; the next operation answers 403 break: authority cached at connect time. Red: the socket keeps receiving deltas after revocation (the R48 control's shape, 200 instead of 403)
    cross-tenant checkpoint read (bind tenant A, read B's checkpoint row) fails closed: zero rows under RLS, or a refused rebind (postgres-connection.js:44-47) break: checkpoint table outside RLS or keyed without the tenant. Red: A reads B's stamp and replays B's rows into A's view
    suppressed timer with the timer stopped and writes continuing, every write-driven alarm still fires; only time-driven alarms (stale data, time-ago) are missed break: the timer also reloads rows (today's timedReloadData). Red: a write-driven alarm is late by one timer interval, or, inversely, stopping the timer loses nothing because the timer was repairing the feed
    lost NOTIFY (drop every notification for an interval) the backstop delivers the cohort within its bound; the event-driven path delivers nothing break: backstop disabled. Red: the cohort is never evaluated until the next write
    rollback inside the transaction no event, no stamp advance, no row already reproduced at the clock head (lower-port "actual rollback", "publish immediately instead of queueing" control: 1 event rather than 0); rerun on the frozen head that joins the clock

    The executable recorded-evidence gates and source applicability are linked from R73 and the queue. They replay the archived measurements rather than starting a fresh native campaign.

    10. Decisions this contract makes¶

    For the reviewer's convenience, the decisions above in one list, each one a sentence the entry bar in §8 can falsify:

    1. The spine is committed rows plus their commit stamp; events are latency.
    2. A checkpoint is a per-tenant, per-consumer database row under the tenant's binding; never process memory.
    3. The interprocess event carries tenant and stamp, or is treated as a wake-up.
    4. Delivery is at-least-once; consumers are idempotent on (tenant, stamp, collection, typed identity).
    5. Progress is by complete equal-stamp cohort, never by page.
    6. Soft deletes are events; hard deletes and raw writers are outside the spine.
    7. Authority and settings are re-read per operation or per cycle, not invalidated by event.
    8. Replay covers crash-after-commit; the publish covers nothing.
    9. Time-only alarm ticks are separate from the later adopted snapshot-reconciliation schedule; a wake or tick alone establishes no recovery guarantee.
    10. Evaluator and notification state is keyed by tenant, settings version and plugin set, with R50's retention policy.
    11. The contract applies only where the commit-coupled stamp exists; the clock (batch B2) is therefore a prerequisite of the first recovery slice, and the clock is absent from the original anchor 35903069. B2 has since joined it locally; R56 owns the joined baseline and its measured limits.

    11. Maintainer acceptance and experiments¶

    The bounded snapshot reconciliation decision selects the initial M5 current-state recovery path. It preserves existing writer contracts and requires explicit row/byte refusal. R73 records bounded functional implementation/native acceptance for the first reference; scan cadence/capacity budgets remain separate measurements. Every-transition and external notification-delivery guarantees retain their own contracts.

    The maintainer adopted B7's design direction on 2026-11-06 in the B7 approval chat: proceed with tenant-local durable checkpoints, commit-coupled ordering, at-least-once replay and idempotent consumers, and use experiments to inform scalability and performance. The decision is recorded in the release decisions. This accepts the direction and the executable entry bar, not measured distributed recovery, a performance budget, a provider, milestone promotion or release admission. R73 supplies the functional M5 disposition and recorded instruments. The queue owns broader item state and remaining admission gates; this design document does not make a fresh current-HEAD or production acceptance claim.

    What UNMEASURED means. The named behavior has no applicable executed capture on the stated assembled source and workload. It is neither a failure nor a pass. A source reading is a hypothesis; a focused positive and mechanism-specific red control support only the schedules and source actually exercised. Existing clock proofs do not establish distributed consumer or notification recovery.

    Correctness experiments before runtime acceptance. Implement the §8 positives and paired breaks in an isolated synthetic producer/consumer slice. Also cut the consumer at each boundary between processing, durable notification handoff and checkpoint persistence: demonstrate no lost accepted durable intent and no second logical request identity for the same retry. Repeated external attempts/effects remain possible after ambiguous acceptance under the adopted retry policy. Provider delivery to a device remains a separate acceptance. Exercise subscription during concurrent commits so startup cannot depend on an uncovered gap between catch-up and listening. Measure an update that overwrites a row, then another update or hard delete before replay: state convergence from current rows is not proof that every intermediate transition can be recovered. Specify which transitions the evaluator must preserve and whether a retained log, outbox or tombstone is required. These are questions for the prototype, not added guarantees.

    Performance experiments before capacity claims. Pin the assembled head, hardware, data size and consumer count; compare clock on/off at identical load. Sweep active and idle tenants, write rate per tenant, burst size, bulk cohorts, reconnect backlog and slow consumers. Include one hot tenant beside ordinary tenants on a shared pool. Capture commit-to-view and commit-to-evaluation p50/p95/p99, clock lock wait/hold time, throughput, pool occupancy, CPU/memory, database reads, checkpoint writes, replay lag/catch-up duration and retained bytes. Evaluate idle poll overhead separately: aggregate work depends on tenants and poll frequency, not only incoming writes. Declare workload-specific freshness and recovery targets before judging an operating envelope; do not invent a tenant capacity from a green functional suite.

    How results change expectations. Correctness positives with meaningful red controls support bounded recovery claims. A load sweep establishes where freshness and recovery targets hold, and where queues grow. Contention may require shorter transactions or admission limits; aggregate polling cost may require batching or a different provider; lost intermediate transitions may require retained history. Per-tenant ordering avoids a global ordering requirement, but shared pools, CPU, retention and database I/O can still couple tenants. Architecture and provider choices remain revisitable when experiments refute their assumptions.
