# Planning the next nominal multitenant run

**Proposed, 2026-11-24.** A nominal run asks whether the expected mix of uploads, reads and viewers receives timely service on a deployment provisioned for that mix. Keep the offer, history, scoring windows and product objectives explicit. A fixed-resource optimization run asks how much one change improves that same workload. A resource increase earns a new deployment result. Credit a code improvement only in a matched comparison. ## Choose the write workload

Run the joined candidate on the expected live offer. Include recent delayed connector records and keep the original reading time when measuring their age. Count connector delivery from its schedule, durable output from API acceptance, and viewer receipt independently. Booting from an attested fixture with deep tenant histories exercises ordinary reads against that data. Reuse a verified historical base across the ladder. Append and verify enough genuine recent observations to cover the planned deadlines once, then restore that child for the points it covers. Record the fixture restore and evaluator restart phases separately. Classify catch-up by the operation and the age of its records. The existing [12-hour outage burst](../../../tools/mt-bench/m7-joined/looping-backlog.js) is a recovery scenario even though its events are recent. For an older backlog or a bulk import/export and restore, measure completed documents, identity and order, recovery time and sustained live service on other tenants. The original clinical timestamps and historical client cursors matter in this scenario. Size serving storage for the retained and imported history. A two-day live writer can still query a tenant with years of data. [Restore and client convergence](multitenancy-decisions.md#restore-history-forward-correction)

## Provision the service and the instrument

**Read-derived 3k trial allocation.** Retain the measured 32 evaluator slices, eight realtime replicas, six API replicas, and eight data-pool seats per API for the first matched comparison. The deployment used two 16-vCPU/64-GiB application workers and a dedicated 16-vCPU/128-GiB database worker. PostgreSQL requested 12 CPUs and 64 GiB and used 32 GiB shared buffers. In the steady phase the cache hit rate was 99.98%, mean block read time was 0.430 ms and there were zero requested checkpoints. The busiest API pool averaged 4.30 of eight seats with zero checkout refusals. Raising its connection limit would also increase work in the frozen backend-identity query.

These are scheduling requests. **Read-derived placement arithmetic** puts the two application workers' case requests at 12.568 and 11.043 CPUs, with 54.82 and 48.10 GiB, on the original Pod placement. Each worker reported 15.86 CPUs and about 57.6 GiB allocatable. Include other Pods, sidecars, DaemonSets, controller placement and per-node free space in the actual render. A larger reservation can improve placement or CPU share under contention. It cannot fix a lost publication or certify peak working set.

- **Driver.** Reconstructing all 118,929 completed iterations gives per-operation concurrency peaks of 4–17. For a 3k trial, pre-initialize about 79 virtual users distributed by operation, using 25% margin over each observed peak. The original preallocation was 27 and the combined maximum 499. Keep cadence and count drops. The observed 38 drops occurred before dispatch. Their cause remains open.
- **Viewers.** At 50 opens/s the minimum ramps are 156, 260 and 520 s. The 3k prime took 173.5 s. Its measured rate projects about 289 s at 5k and 578 s at 10k before any allowance for jitter. Reserve separate setup time, retain the 4-CPU/10-GiB controller as a 3k starting point and check memory, ports and evidence space for the actual offer. Around 8,119 established source ports served 7,800 viewers in steady state. The larger point projects about 27,063 on one destination pair versus 28,232 in the original range. The campaign already has an observer-port-range control and a larger evidence-store render. Multiple observer sources are another option if measured source limits interfere.
- **Fixture and clocks.** Select a catalogued synthetic fixture with enough remaining clinical history for restore, catch-up, viewer priming, ordinary load and the relevant drain. Preflight the exact native work deadline before allocating a case. Give collection and cleanup their own budgets. The 3k gate took 429.4 s, including a 300 s quiet-window convention. Replaying the frozen sampler with a 30 s confirmation admitted load at 153.3 s instead of 428.7 s. A synthetic return of pending work at 214.6 s escaped the short check and prevented the long check from clearing in the recorded interval. Budget the existing check for the next nominal comparison. An earlier-start discovery run should continue observing backlog through settling and qualify its steady interval separately. Keep cold/restart results visible and start the clinical clock at acceptance for every accepted input, including during warm-up.
- **Time budget.** As an initial 3k planning allowance, reserve 20 minutes for setup from case entry, 40 minutes for ordinary traffic, 10 minutes for drain and collection, and 10 minutes for cleanup. The observed 3k foundation-to-first-request time was 12 min 42 s. At the observed prime rate, 10k needs roughly seven additional minutes just to open its viewers. Recalculate fixture eligibility and scheduling from actual phase deadlines. This example is separate from any live campaign grant.
- **Larger points.** Recompute the app placement, viewer-source port and memory demand, driver concurrency and database CPU/statement/connection demand at each offer. Use the measured 3k per-role demand as a starting estimate. Allow sufficient workers for the requests to fit with ordinary system overhead. A larger fleet or independently scaled observer establishes a new nominal operating point. Keep a fixed-allocation replay for the optimization comparison.

## Score the outcomes independently

**Recommendation.** Publish whether the workload completed, what users received, which product objectives it met and which evidence was unavailable. Record the strict HTTP result even when another measurement passes. At 3k, 13/89,202 steady responses were 503 (0.0146%, within the 0.1% aggregate objective), the zero steady storage-refusal target failed, and the busiest data pool exceeded its 50% planning target. Preserve those three facts even when the stage summary records only its first failure.

Keep stored-write reconciliation, tenant authority, isolation, restore fencing and datastore health. Retain 30 s durable p95 at expected load, roughly 1 s stretch and the 120 s clinical ceiling from acceptance. Score browser freshness separately using 4/10/30 s bands, upper tails, unmatched and censored counts. Keep per-route HTTP p50/p95/p99, refusal phase, offered/issued/completed throughput, drops and end backlog. The 3k wrong-host socket probes received transport 403 before the application callback. Application-level isolation on those probes is unmeasured. For optimization, keep fixture content and clinical age, active roster, per-route offer, viewer distribution, topology, allocations and warm-up comparable. Build supported simplifications concurrently, run their affected checks and try the joined configuration at the target offer. Use a matched replay of one mechanism when a failed outcome or a specific performance claim needs attribution. Qualify the frozen candidate once. [Qualification sequence](multitenancy-decisions.md#predictable-fixes-then-test)

For a nominal deployment, reserve a realistic envelope in advance and report actual usage and spare capacity. Reducing upload cadence, dropping viewer updates, serving stale status or delaying authority rechecks would each change what users receive and would require its own product decision. The measurements support implementation and harness changes first.
