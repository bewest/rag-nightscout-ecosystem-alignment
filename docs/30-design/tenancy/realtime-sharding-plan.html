    Realtime sharding, composition and recovery¶

    Living document, last changed 2026-11-15 at 268046679.

    Contributor-facing living guide, updated 2026-11-15. Read-derived current source: application 8ddcc01208c11c18145c44c1076ae4c91f79ffa2, deployment 23e73da7ad8d57bb0a360c29b3ef2320ed229238. Fixed-roster realtime sharding and controlled moves are implemented on local branches. Historical native evidence keeps its exact source below; it does not qualify this later candidate or prove horizontal capacity. Queue home: MT-REALTIME-SHARDING. The runtime topology owns current composition; the release contents own joined qualification and publication state.

    Implemented boundary¶

    Each tenant has one admitted realtime owner for connections, projection work and notification handoff. Stable logical shards group tenants. A separate versioned map places those shards on workers. Changing worker count does not change the logical shard count. Evaluator assignment remains independent.

    The ordinary deployment uses one realtime process when placement is unselected. The optional fixed-roster binding renders realtime workers, their private addresses, backend assignment, admitted routes, edge routing and lifecycle participants together. Selecting more workers supplies this composition; capacity and a particular platform binding still require measurement.

    flowchart LR
  API[DATA API] --> PG[(PostgreSQL)]
  PG --> Stream[Maintained pgstream]
  Stream --> EV[Evaluator]
  EV --> Output[(Committed completed output)]
  Output --> RT[Assigned realtime owner]
  Client[Client] --> Edge[Consumer edge]
  Edge --> RT
  RT --> Client
  Backend[Backend placement] --> RT
  Routes[Admitted route view] --> Edge
  Routes --> Alarm[Evaluator alarm adapter]
  Alarm --> RT

    Realtime now consumes committed evaluator output and builds its client projection and frames. The old description of two independent clinical acquisition paths is superseded by the maintained communication contract. A completed acquisition, committed output, client frame and notification handoff remain different outcomes.

    Component Responsibility and source home

    Owner resource Holds an actual datastore resource for the stable consumer and selected worker/assignment contract. Loss revokes work. lib/realtime-placement/owner.js.
    Realtime runtime Selects assigned registry tenants, stages warm state privately and checks current ownership, activity and settings before activation and effects. lib/server/serve/preview-realtime.js and lib/server/realtime/runtime.js.

    The exact fields, owner-key versions, private contract versions and publisher callbacks live in core lib/realtime-placement/README.md. Consumers must use the matching core/deployment contract rather than copy a map or transport schema.

    Logical roles and process placement¶

    REST v1/v2/v3 are one logical request-serving component. Each real replica still owns pools and in-flight operations and participates in maintenance. Realtime owns live connections and process-local projection state; its durable checkpoints remain in PostgreSQL. A replacement reconstructs current state and clients establish new sessions. Evaluator receipts, completed output and current ownership also survive independently of its process-local working state.

    These responsibilities do not select a Kubernetes workload kind. A platform binding must map every declared worker to actual process identity, private addresses and consumer routes, prove old-owner exclusion and wait for target readiness. An ordinal or assignment UUID alone proves none of those conditions. The existing evidence below covers its declared native/Compose bindings; it does not establish a Kubernetes or multi-host deployment.

    Co-location remains a deployment choice. Separate role contexts and ownership must survive shared processes or Pods, and clinical scheduling must retain its budget under viewer load. Starting identical realtime configurations behind a load balancer does not create admitted ownership or complete notification routing.

    Placement, routing and durable state¶

    The backend placement view describes intended ownership. A separate admitted route view serves the edge and evaluator alarm adapter. Backend/cohort readers use the authoritative assignment; publishing a consumer route does not change maintenance coverage or make a cold owner ready.

    A tenant is selected by canonical tenant identity. Fixed worker destinations are validated private instance addresses, not client-selected URLs. Polling, WebSocket upgrades and main/storage/alarm namespaces retain process affinity for a live connection. Backend admission checks current assignment and authority; the route map grants no client permissions.

    A realtime owner retains the stable durable consumer identity across restarts and moves. The actual held owner resource, assignment contract and fresh process observation are distinct from a reusable worker name. Supported owner-key versions have explicit compatibility rules; a binding must select the same version in its runtime, routes and cohort.

    Registry admission and placement changes can stage new assigned tenants. Cold or failed preparation stays outside healthy scheduling until current activity, settings, membership and ownership checks pass. Empty and all-deferred workers remain inspectable. Paused or suspended tenants do not gain fabricated clinical progress merely because their worker is live.

    Controlled moves and failure recovery¶

    The implemented publisher supports controlled fixed-roster moves and consumes independently confirmed old-owner exclusion during recovery. Its sequence is:

    1. Hold the publication fence and validate the actual prior map, complete registry revision and affected tenant operation windows. A pinned restore excludes a conflicting move.
    2. Stop and drain affected old owners through the trusted platform callback. Verify exact old-process termination or effective fencing. Route removal, connection loss and lease age alone do not establish exclusion.
    3. Publish the next backend generation, start the declared replacement and warm its actual assigned state while retaining the fence.
    4. Wait for eligible target readiness, then publish matching consumer/alarm routes and commit. Clients reconnect, reauthorize and recover current state.

    Unchanged shards can continue while a target warms. A cold target refuses connections and alarm effects. A failed or partly published move retains uncertainty; recovery uses a fresh admitted generation. It does not restore an old map, stopped incarnation or checkpoint to simulate rollback success.

    If the old owner may still be alive, the affected shard remains unavailable. Automatic takeover under an ambiguous partition needs a separate effective-fence contract and adversarial proof. Process termination cannot recall bytes already sent to a socket.

    The source cohort supports realtime tenant subsets alongside full API coverage. It includes real idle and draining workers, pins the placement revision and checks actual datastore/backend identity. Missing owners or unknown clinical backends refuse; a sampled endpoint or self-reported subset does not prove completeness. Current pause, suspension, deletion and authority checks still apply during moves.

    Client and alarm semantics¶

    A main-namespace full frame recovers current state. Storage subscriptions also need their supported collection/history reload ordering; an acknowledgement is not itself that reload. Reconnection does not replay every alarm a device missed. Alarm handoff acceptance is distinct from confirmation that a device received it.

    A move preserves durable notification request/attempt identity. A late old-owner response belongs to its original attempt. A lost response remains ambiguous; changing the address does not justify an immediate transparent retry or synthetic success. Accepted handoffs are not made pending again solely because an owner moved. Durable per-device replay would be a separate protocol and state capability.
