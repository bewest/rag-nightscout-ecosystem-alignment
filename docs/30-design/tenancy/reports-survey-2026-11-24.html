    1. Clinical standards these reports mirror [C]¶

    • 2019 International Consensus on Time in Range (Battelino et al., Diabetes Care 42(8):1593–1603, doi:10.2337/dci19-0028, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6973648/):
    • Data: at least 14 days of CGM with at least 70% of the data captured.
    • Bands: TBR level 2 below 54 mg/dL; TBR level 1 54–69; TIR 70–180; TAR level 1 181–250; TAR level 2 above 250.
    • Adult targets: TIR above 70%, TBR below 4% (below 1% under 54), TAR below 25% (below 5% over 250).
    • Other targets: CV of 36% or less. In pregnancy the range is 63–140 mg/dL.
    • Reporting: a standardised AGP report.
    • GMI (Bergenstal et al., Diabetes Care 41(11):2275–2280, 2018): GMI (%) = 3.31 + 0.02392 × mean glucose (mg/dL). The older eA1c formula, (mean + 46.7) / 28.7 (ADAG, Nathan et al. 2008), is still used by several apps.
    • AGP: the current AGP report plots the 5/25/50/75/95 percentiles by time of day, "as if in a single day" (sample report, https://www.healthpartners.com/institute/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Ambulatory-Glucose-Profile-Report-Overview.pdf). Older AGP renderings use 10/25/50/75/90, and several apps below still do.
    • Comprehensive Glucose Pentagon (CGP): Vigersky et al. 2018, doi:10.1177/1932296817718561, as cited in cgm-remote-monitor lib/report/pentagon.js:3.

    What the standards imply for storage: every standard metric is computed over a 14–90-day window of whole local days, and it is dominated by mean, SD and band fractions. The one part that is not a daily scalar is the AGP, a per-time-of-day percentile pooled across days.

    2. cgm-remote-monitor reports (official/dev 79144cbd0) [R]¶

    These are shared mechanics for every report: - Range: the default is the last 7 days (lib/report/reportclient.js:92). Presets run 1/2/3/7/14/31/90 days (views/reportindex.html:52-58). The hard cap is maxdays = 6*31 = 186 days (reportclient.js:35, :468, :493), and the day before the range is also loaded for treatments (:504-515). - Loading: day by day, three API v1 calls per day: - entries.json?find[date][$gte..$lt]&count=10000 (:698) - treatments.json?find[created_at]…&count=1000 (:758) - devicestatus.json (:794), only when the IOB, COB, OpenAPS or predicted options are on (:674) - Profiles come from /api/v1/profiles by startDate (:855-889), and Profile Switch treatments from :818. - Day definition: a report day is a calendar day in the profile time zone, DST included (lib/report/agp-data.js:5-10). - Deduplication: - SGVs are deduplicated to at most one per minute (lib/report/uniqsgv.js:21). - AGP data also drops repeated timestamps (agp-data.js:16-18). - Values outside 39–600 are dropped (agp-data.js:15). - Statistics library: mean; population SD; and a nearest-rank quantile that averages the two middle values for an even count (lib/report/statistics.js:31-63). - Targets: taken from the user's input (reportclient.js:278), with defaults bgTargetBottom and bgTargetTop (:48-53). The low/in/high split in several reports therefore depends on a per-request threshold.

    The "needs detail" column means: can this be produced at full fidelity from a per-day summary row of the kind in section 5?

    5. Proposed per-tenant-day summary row¶

    The design rests on three rules: - Mergeable fields only: counts, sums, sums of squares, fixed-bin histograms, and per-day extrema used as per-day series. A day range then merges by addition. - Keyed by (tenant, local_date, tz_id). The day is computed in the profile zone with DST, as in agp-data.js:5-10, and rebuilt when the zone changes (nighttroop job.go:96). - Built from the same deduplication as the reports (uniqsgv.js, one SGV per minute, values 39–600), so a summary and a detail read agree.

    group fields approx. bytes
    key and meta tenant, local_date, tz_id, utc_offset_min, schema_version, built_at, source_srvModified_max, stale flag 40
    coverage n_sgv, observed_minutes (time-weighted, intervals capped at 5 min, agp-data.js:31-35), n_mbg, n_cal, n_invalid (<39 or >600) 20
    glucose moments Σbg and Σbg² by count; Σbg·t and Σbg²·t by time (pentagon.js:66-79); min, max 40
    glucose histogram counts per 1 mg/dL bin over 39–400, plus under/over; sparse or varint-encoded ~150–300 compressed (724 raw as uint16)
    standard bands by time minutes below 54, 54–69, 70–180, 181–250, above 250, 70–140, 63–140 28
    CGP / AUC lowAuc, lowTime, highAuc, highTime (pentagon.js:69-71) 16
    path / variability per-day GVI path length and ideal time, Σ|Δbg|, n steps, t1/t2 rate counts, first and last (bg, t) (glucosedistribution.js:543-600); max low-risk and max high-risk for ADRR 48
    episodes count of hypo episodes below 54 and below 70, plus an episode-open-at-midnight flag 6
    5-minute trace 288 × uint16 slot values (newest or mean per slot, 0 for empty), as in nighttroop and loopalyzer slots ~350 compressed (576 raw)
    per-hour glucose 24 × (n, Σ) 192 raw, ~100 compressed
    insulin and food bolus_u, smb_u, basal_scheduled_u, basal_delivered_u (+temp, −temp), carbs_g, protein_g, fat_g, carb_insulin_u (AAPS); counts of boluses, carb entries, temp basals; site, sensor and insulin change counts 60
    per-hour insulin and carbs 24 × (bolus_u, basal_u, carbs_g) ~150 compressed
    optional loop series 288-slot IOB and COB (nighttroop loop_iob/loop_cob), int16 at 0.01 U / 1 g ~700 compressed

    Size [R, estimated, not measured]: - About 1.0–1.3 KB per tenant-day without the loop series and about 2 KB with it, roughly 1–2% of the ~0.13 MB per tenant-day cold-bundle figure in proposal §5.4. - A year is about 0.4–0.7 MB per tenant. - Dropping the trace and per-hour arrays leaves a scalar-plus-histogram core of about 0.4 KB.

    5.1 Exact from the row (merge by addition) [R]¶

    • Coverage and count statistics: readings/day, % CGM active, 14-day/70% sufficiency (time-weighted).
    • Moments and indices:
    • mean, SD and CV over any day range, count- or time-weighted, from n, Σ and Σ²
    • GMI and eA1c from the mean
    • J-index from the mean and SD
    • Bands:
    • all standard bands
    • any user target band (cgm-remote-monitor's per-request rp_targetlow/high; reporter's per-day targets) from the 1 mg/dL histogram
    • GRI from the band fractions
    • Pooled distribution statistics: pooled median and quartiles, band medians, the glucose distribution histogram, out-of-range RMS against any target, LBGI/HBGI. All are exact because SGV is an integer in mg/dL, so the histogram is lossless for the pooled multiset. In display mmol the same holds after the existing conversion.
    • Per-day aggregates:
    • CGP pentagon exactly as pentagon.js computes it
    • ADRR (a mean of per-day maxima)
    • TDD with basal/bolus split, carbs/protein/fat per day and averages
    • hourly means (reporter's daily hours)
    • daytoday's distribution totals
    • AAPS- and Trio-style TDD history
    • Calendar-aligned period rollups, such as week and month rows and comparison periods 1–12 months back.

    statistic from what fidelity
    AGP and time-of-day percentiles across days (agp 15 min, percentile 30 min, hourlystats 1 h, reporter ~288 bins, Trio/LoopFollow/GlycemicGPT) the 5-minute trace
    • exact for reporter, whose input is already a 5-minute grid
    • close for cgm-remote-monitor, whose 15- and 30-minute bins would hold 3–6 grid values per day instead of 3–15 raw readings
    • diverges for 1-minute sensors
    Daily percentiles — non-mergeable; storing p5..p95 per day does not give multi-day percentiles
    Exact multi-day AGP a per-day × per-time-bin 1 mg/dL histogram: 96 × sparse ≈ 1–2 KB/day is the alternative if exactness matters more than size

    Hypo episode counts across days episode counts plus the midnight-carry flag nearly exact

    5.3 Not reproducible from daily aggregates: detail needed [R]¶

    Reports that need detail: - Day-by-day charts: - cgm-remote-monitor: daytoday, weektoweek, the 28-day AGP mini-grid - reporter: daily graphic and daily analysis - The trace gives a 5-minute rendition, but not the raw points, mbg markers or the de-spiking interpolation (glucosedistribution.js:281-359). - loopalyzer meal time-shift. It needs carb timestamps and the IOB/COB series. The optional loop series plus treatment detail cover it. - Treatment lists and edits: cgm-remote-monitor treatments, reporter daily log, and food/notes/eventType searches (reportclient.js:342-417). These are documents. - calibrations. It needs raw filtered/unfiltered and cal entries. - Predictions and OpenAPS overlays read from devicestatus (loopalyzer.js:466-473, daytoday options). - Audit and v1/v3 reads of individual documents.

    Implication for tiers [R, not measured]: - Glucose statistics over long spans can be served from summary rows at any age. - Charts and lists need detail for the days displayed. Users ask for those at 1–14 days, and occasionally up to the 90–186-day range caps; that is the hot and warm tiers, with cold reads acceptable at seconds. - Treatments are small next to entries and devicestatus and back several "need detail" reports, so treatment detail is a candidate for longer retention than entries/devicestatus detail. This is unmeasured; confirm with per-collection bytes.

    5.4 Design cautions [R]¶

    • Thresholds differ across tools:
    • cgm-remote-monitor dailystats: low <low, high >=high (dailystats.js:110-115)
    • cgm-remote-monitor agp: 70–180 inclusive (agp.js:130)
    • AAPS: ≥39 and inclusive bounds (TirCalculatorImpl.kt:84-102)
    • A 1 mg/dL histogram serves all of them. Precomputed band columns serve only the convention used to build them.
    • Weighting differs: count-weighted (cgm-remote-monitor bands, AAPS) versus time-weighted (pentagon.js, % active). Keep both moment sets.
    • SD differs: population SD (cgm-remote-monitor, reporter, LoopFollow) versus sample SD with N−1 (Trio). Both follow from n, Σ and Σ².
    • Rows go stale. Backfill and edits must mark days stale and rebuild them, as nighttroop does with stale_at (job.go:210-212). A cold-tier edit that promotes a document to hot (proposal §5.4) must also mark its day stale.
    • Built from detail only. Summary rows must be produced from detail before any detail-deletion limit takes effect. Once detail is gone, a rebuild (new statistic, bug fix) is impossible. schema_version records which fields a row carries.
