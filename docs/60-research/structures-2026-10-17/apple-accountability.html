    How Apple's developer program apportions accountability¶

    Living document, last changed 2026-10-17 at 8869dd3be.

    For the Nightscout Foundation board, the maintainers of the ecosystem projects, and the community. Research note, 2026-10-17. Every fact below was read from the cited page on 2026-10-17 unless it says otherwise; Apple changes these pages often (the App Review Guidelines were last updated the day before this note), so re-read the source before quoting a figure.

    Draft for legal review. Wherever this note characterises a contract, a statute or a regulator's requirement (§2, §3 and the "needs review" column of §6), it is a non-lawyer's reading, to be checked by counsel, and for §6 also by a regulatory or quality professional, before anyone relies on it. The specific points for a reviewer are listed in §8.

    It adds to PARTNER-READINESS (tiers A, B and C of access to a device, and §7 on credentials), DIGITAL-RIGHTS (rights that do not depend on a manufacturer's permission), PRECEDENTS (open-source organisations; Apple is not among them because it is a platform owner, not a commons) and ECOSYSTEM-PROGRAMME §2b (sponsoring versus operating). Corrections to the repository's TestFlight and App Store pages are in §7.

    Terms used¶

    term meaning here
    Apple Account the personal login a person uses for all Apple services
    personal team what Xcode (Apple's development tool) calls an Apple Account that is not in a paid program; it can install apps the person builds onto their own devices
    provisioning profile a small signed file from Apple that lists which app, which devices and which special permissions a build may use; without a valid one, an iPhone will not install or launch a self-built app
    certificate the cryptographic identity a developer signs builds with; Apple issues it and can revoke it
    entitlement / capability a permission written into the app and the profile that unlocks a system feature (health data, push notifications, alerts that sound when the phone is muted). A managed capability is one Apple must grant to the account on request
    App Review Apple's human and automated review of each app submission against its App Review Guidelines
    TestFlight Apple's beta-testing service: builds uploaded to Apple are installed by invited testers through the TestFlight app
    notarization an automated Apple malware and signing check. On the Mac it applies to software distributed outside the Mac App Store; in the EU it is also a reduced review for iPhone apps distributed outside the App Store
    D-U-N-S Number a nine-digit identifier that Dun & Bradstreet, a business-information company, assigns to an organisation; Apple uses it to check that an organisation exists and is a legal entity
    DMA the EU Digital Markets Act, a regulation that obliges very large platforms ("gatekeepers") to allow, among other things, other app stores and payment methods
    DSA the EU Digital Services Act, which among other things obliges online marketplaces to verify and display the contact details of businesses ("traders") selling through them
    DPLA the Apple Developer Program License Agreement, the contract every paid member signs
    indemnify to promise to cover another party's losses, including legal costs, if a claim arises from what you did

    1. The tiers, as Apple's pages describe them today¶

    1.1 Free Apple Account development (personal team)¶

    • [T1] Limits. "You can register up to 10 App IDs, which expire after 7 days. You can register up to 3 devices, which expire after 7 days. You can install up to 3 apps per device. Provisioning profiles that enable apps to be installed on a device will expire 7 days from issuance. You'll need to rebuild and reinstall your app to your device after expiration." Apple adds that "you'll be required to reprovision your apps to a device periodically." (developer account overview; the old address developer.apple.com/support/compare-memberships/ redirects there.)
    • [T2] What the free tier gets. The same page's table gives free registrants beta Xcode and OS releases, on-device testing with Xcode, the forums, Feedback Assistant and Meet with Apple activities, and not code-level support, Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles, Mac notarization, App Store Connect, TestFlight or Xcode Cloud.
    • [T3] Capabilities. Apple's supported capabilities (iOS) table lists what a free account ("Apple Developer: … No cost is associated with this agreement and developers can't distribute apps") may put in a profile. Available free: App groups, Background modes, Data protection, HealthKit, HomeKit, Inter-App Audio, Keychain sharing, Maps, Wireless Accessory Configuration. Not available free, among others: Push notifications, Time Sensitive Notifications, Near Field Communication (NFC) Tag Reading, HealthKit Estimate Recalibration, iCloud, Associated domains, App Attest, Sign in with Apple.
    • [T4] Managed capabilities, Critical Alerts among them, are out of reach of the free tier. Requests go through Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles and "your Account Holder will need to submit your request" (capability requests); Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles is not available to free accounts [T2]. This is an inference from two pages; Apple does not state it in one sentence.
    • [T5] The licence. Free development runs under the Xcode and Apple SDKs Agreement (PDF marked EA2002; xcode.pdf, file modified 2026-08-03) and the Apple Developer Agreement (dated March 18, 2025; English PDF). Section 2.2.B of the Xcode agreement lets a developer use provisioning profiles to install apps on "a reasonable, limited number of Authorized Test Units … only for internal testing and development of Your Applications, or for Your own personal, non-commercial use." Section 2.2.A: "You may not distribute any Applications developed using the Apple SDKs (excluding the macOS SDK) absent entering into a separate written agreement with Apple." "Provisioning Profiles" are defined as "time-limited files" (§1).

    1.2 Apple Developer Program¶

    • [P1] Fee. "The Apple Developer Program annual fee is 99 USD and the Apple Developer Enterprise Program annual fee is 299 USD, in local currency where available. Prices may vary by region." (program enrollment; also "$99 annual membership" on programs.)
    • [P2] Individual or organisation. Enrolment needs an Apple Account with two-factor authentication and the legal age of majority. An individual's "personal legal name will be listed as the seller on the App Store". An organisation must be a legal entity ("We don't accept DBAs, fictitious businesses, trade names, or branches"), have a D-U-N-S Number ("Not required for government organizations"), and be enrolled by someone with "the legal authority to bind your organization to legal agreements", with a work email on the organisation's domain and a functioning public website. Apple may ask for notarised business documents (same page; D-U-N-S: "your business must be recognized as a legal entity … to enter into the legal terms and obligations of Apple Developer Program agreements").
    • [P3] Devices. Members "can register up to 100 of the following devices, per product family, per membership year"; disabling a device does not free a slot; the count is restored only at the start of a membership year (devices overview).
    • [P4] Expiry of membership. "If your Apple Developer Program membership expires, your apps will no longer be available for download … However, your apps will still function for users who have already installed or downloaded them." For the Enterprise Program, installed software "will continue to run for 90 days after the expiration date" (renewal).
    • [P5] Check-in with Apple. For memberships created after June 6, 2021, development- and ad hoc-signed apps "check in with the PPQ service when the app is first launched … If the device can't successfully make a connection, the app may not launch." An offline profile "valid for 7 days" is available (provisioning profile updates).

    1.3 Fee waivers¶

    • [W1] Eligibility (fee waivers): "Be a legal entity with a status as a nonprofit organization, accredited educational institution, or government entity; Not be an individual, sole proprietor, or single-person business; Not have signed the Paid Applications Agreement … to offer paid apps or in-app purchases …; and Not otherwise sell digital goods or services through any of your apps." A nonprofit "must be officially recognized by a relevant authority in your region of operation. For example, the Internal Revenue Service in the U.S." The Account Holder re-confirms eligibility at each renewal; the waiver is removed if requirements stop being met. The waiver is an option inside organisation enrolment, so the D-U-N-S and legal-entity requirements [P2] still apply.
    • [W2] Inconsistency. The waiver page still says that an approved organisation that has signed "the Alternative Terms Addendum for Apps in the EU" will not pay "the Core Technology Fee". Apple's EU page [E1] says that addendum was phased out and the Core Technology Fee replaced on October 1,
    • The two pages disagree; the EU page is the more recent statement.

    1.4 Apple Developer Enterprise Program¶

    • [N1] (enterprise) "299 USD per membership year". "Only for the internal use and distribution of proprietary apps in specific use cases that are not adequately addressed with public apps on the App Store, custom apps through Apple Business or Ad Hoc distribution, or beta testing through TestFlight." The organisation must "Have 100 or more employees", be a legal entity with a D-U-N-S Number, "Have systems in place to ensure only employees can download your internal-use apps", and "Participate in and pass Apple's verification interview and continuous evaluation process". Verification is repeated at renewal; "Apple reserves the right to reject your application at its sole discretion."
    • [N2] Enterprise members do not get App Store Connect, TestFlight or Xcode Cloud [T2]. The Enterprise licence agreement itself was not read for this note.

    1.5 TestFlight¶

    • [F1] "You can test a build for up to 90 days." "Your build becomes unavailable for testers after 90 days." Up to 10,000 external testers and up to 100 internal testers who are App Store Connect users with a role on the team. "When you add the first build of your app to a group, the build gets sent to App Review … A review is required only for the first build. Subsequent builds may not require a full review." At upload, Apple asks the developer to determine whether app encryption documentation is needed for US export compliance (TestFlight overview).
    • [F2] "You can share up to 100 builds." External testing needs "your first build already approved by App Review for TestFlight. Your builds are automatically sent for review once they're added to a group" (TestFlight). Whether every external build is reviewed, or only the first, is stated differently on the two pages.
    • [F3] Contract terms (DPLA §7.4): internal distribution is "solely for their internal use in testing, evaluating and/or developing Your Applications"; testers may not be charged; and "You may not use TestFlight for purposes that are not related to improving the quality, performance, or usability of pre-release versions of Your Application (e.g., continuous distribution of demo versions of Your Application in an attempt to circumvent the App Store …)".

    1.6 Entitlements that need approval¶

    • [X1] Critical Alerts. Apple's entitlement documentation: an app with com.apple.developer.usernotifications.critical-alerts "can request [criticalAlert] authorization to receive push notifications that cause the system to play a sound even when the app is locked, muted, or a person uses Do Not Disturb focus"; the authorisation-options documentation says "Critical alerts require a special entitlement issued by Apple." The request form (developer.apple.com/contact/request/notifications-critical-alerts-entitlement/) requires sign-in and was not read; Apple's approval criteria are therefore not verified here. Secondary sources describe them as health, medical, home-security and public-safety uses.
    • [X2] HealthKit is a self-service capability, available even to free accounts [T3]; the obligations attach through the contract and the guidelines (§2, [R3]–[R5]), not through approval.
    • [X3] The guidelines' introduction: "Some features and technologies that are not generally available to developers may be offered as an entitlement for limited use cases."

    1.7 App Review Guidelines on health¶

    From the App Review Guidelines, "Last Updated: October 16, 2026". Sections marked on the page as applying to both App Store review and notarization review are marked (N).

    • [R1] 1.4.1 (N) "Medical apps that could provide inaccurate data or information, or that could be used for diagnosing or treating patients may be reviewed with greater scrutiny." Apps "must clearly disclose data and methodology to support accuracy claims relating to health measurements"; apps claiming to measure "blood glucose levels … using only the sensors on the device are not permitted"; "Apps should remind users to check with a doctor"; "If your medical app has received regulatory clearance, please submit a link to that documentation with your app."
    • [R2] 1.4.2 (N) "Drug dosage calculators must come from the drug manufacturer, a hospital, university, health insurance company, pharmacy or other approved entity, or receive approval by the FDA or one of its international counterparts. Given the potential harm to patients, we need to be sure that the app will be supported and updated over the long term."
    • [R3] 5.1.1(ix) (N) "Apps that provide services in highly regulated fields (such as … healthcare …) or that require sensitive user information should be submitted by a legal entity that provides the services, and not by an individual developer." 5.1.1(i) requires a privacy policy that explains retention, deletion and how consent is revoked.
    • [R4] 5.1.2(vi) (N) Data from HealthKit and the Clinical Health Records API "may not be used for marketing, advertising or use-based data mining, including by third parties."
    • [R5] 5.1.3 (N) Health data may not be used or disclosed to third parties for advertising or data mining; apps "must not write false or inaccurate data into HealthKit … and may not store personal health information in iCloud"; research apps need participant consent and "approval from an independent ethics review board".
    • [R6] Documentation. "Medical apps that make diagnostic or treatment claims: Regulatory clearance for each country or region where the app is available, such as FDA, CE, or CFDA documentation, or a supporting test report or peer-reviewed study" (complete review).
    • [R7] Regulated medical device status (new in 2026). Announced March 26, 2026: apps distributed in the EEA, UK or US whose primary or secondary category is Health & Fitness or Medical, or that mark "frequent" medical or treatment information in the age-rating questionnaire, must declare whether they are a regulated medical device. Required for new apps from that date; existing apps "must provide a status by early 2027", after which updates cannot be submitted (news). A "Yes" requires an EU SRN or FDA Owner/Operator Number, an instructions-for-use URL, the indications-for-use or intended-purpose statement and safety information, all shown on the product page (declare status). Self-certification under the EU MDR or UK regulations counts as "Yes".

    1.8 Notarization on the Mac¶

    • [M1] "Notarization of macOS software is not App Review. The Apple notary service is an automated system that scans your software for malicious content, checks for code-signing issues". Since macOS 10.15, "all software built after June 1, 2019, and distributed with Developer ID must be notarized." The service "maintains an audit trail of the software distributed using your signing key", and tickets can be revoked (notarizing macOS software). Notarization is not available to free accounts [T2].

    1.9 The EU: alternative distribution and trader status¶

    • [E1] Unified terms from October 1, 2026. "The Apple Developer Program License Agreement, updated August 18, 2026, introduced new business terms … Effective October 1, 2026 … The Core Technology Fee, a per-install fee for developers who achieve extraordinary scale, has been replaced by the Core Technology Commission, a simple 5% commission on digital transactions in apps distributed outside the App Store." The Initial Acquisition Fee and Store Services Fee are eliminated; the earlier EU addenda are "superseded by Attachment 14" (apps in the EU, which now redirects to /support/apps-in-the-eu). The commission applies to sales; the page lists no per-install charge for free apps.
    • [E2] Notarization for iPhone apps. "A baseline review that applies to all apps, regardless of their distribution channel, focused on platform policies for security and privacy", combining "automated checks and human review", against the Notarization Review Guidelines, a subset of the App Review Guidelines (accuracy, functionality, safety, security, privacy). "If Apple determines that an app contains known malware after it's been installed, it will be prevented from launching."
    • [E3] Authorised marketplaces and Web Distribution need Apple's authorisation. Eligibility (any one of): a "moderate financial-stability bar as scored by Dun & Bradstreet"; publicly traded; venture funding; an audit by a licensed accountant; "a government entity, educational institution, or nonprofit approved for a fee waiver"; a "stand-by letter of credit of USD 1,000,000"; or one million first annual installs. "Companies are no longer required to have a legal entity or be established in the EU." A marketplace operator holding a fee waiver may distribute only apps from developers holding the same waiver [W1].
    • [E4] EU App Store commission (same page): 26% on Apple In-App Purchase (15% for Small Business Program participants and subscriptions after year one); 20% or 10% with alternative payment processing.
    • [E5] DSA trader status. "Articles 30 and 31 of the Digital Services Act (DSA) require Apple to verify and display trader contact information for all traders distributing apps on the App Store in the European Union". Every developer must declare a status, even without EU distribution; "Apple can't determine whether you're a trader"; a hobbyist "may not be considered a trader"; "if you have a legal status associated with a business activity, that would suggest you may be a trader." Individuals who are traders publish an address, phone and email; organisations publish the D-U-N-S address. Non-traders' EU customers are told consumer-protection rights will not apply (DSA trader requirements).

    1.10 Small Business Program¶

    • [S1] "A reduced commission rate of 15% on paid apps and Apple In-App Purchases" for developers with up to 1 million USD in proceeds in the prior calendar year, counted together with any "Associated Developer Accounts" (over 50% common ownership); requires accepting the Paid Apps agreement (Small Business Program). The standard non-EU rate was not read for this note.

    1.11 Age assurance¶

    • [G1] Texas SB2420: from January 1, 2026, new Texas accounts face "age assurance and parent or guardian consent on behalf of minors under the age of 18 for downloads, purchases, and significant changes associated with an app"; "It's the developer's responsibility to determine when there's a significant change to their app" (Nov 4, 2025). On June 3, 2026, after "a recent court ruling lifting an injunction", the requirements took effect June 4, 2026 (news). Utah (May 6, 2026) and Louisiana (July 1, 2026) use the same tools; 18+ apps are blocked in Australia, Brazil and Singapore without adult confirmation from February 24, 2026 (news). The status of the Texas law after June 3, 2026 was not verified.

    2. What each tier obliges, and what Apple takes on¶

    The agreements are on Apple's agreements page: DPLA "Last update: August 18, 2026"; Paid Applications Agreement (Schedules 2 and 3) "December 17, 2025"; Apple Developer Agreement March 18, 2025. DPLA text read from the DPLA page.

    topic free tier (Xcode and Apple SDKs Agreement; Apple Developer Agreement) paid program (DPLA)
    who is responsible "You … will be solely responsible for all costs, expenses, losses and liabilities incurred … Your related development and limited distribution efforts" (Xcode §2.1); "solely responsible for all user assistance, warranty and support" (§2.2.B) Schedule 1, Exhibit B, item 5: the end-user licence must say "You, not Apple, are responsible for addressing any claims … including … (i) product liability claims; (ii) any claim that the Licensed Application fails to conform to any applicable legal or regulatory requirement"; ad hoc distribution: "solely responsible for all user assistance, warranty and support" (§7.3)
    indemnification developer indemnifies Apple for breach and for "Your use of the Apple Software … Your development of Applications, or Your loading of Applications" (Xcode §4) seven heads of claim, including any claim by end users, parents or authorities "regarding Your Covered Products" (§10(v))
    life-critical use Apple software "is not intended for use in the development of Applications in which errors … could lead to death, personal injury, or severe physical or environmental damage", and the developer indemnifies Apple for such use (Xcode §4) the same acknowledgement and indemnity (§10, second paragraph); §12: not "suitable for use in situations … where errors, delays, failures or inaccuracies … could lead to death, personal injury"
    limit of Apple's liability "In no event shall Apple's total liability … exceed the amount of fifty dollars ($50.00)" (Xcode §7; Apple Developer Agreement §14) the same $50 cap, "other than as may be required by applicable law in cases involving personal injury" (§13)
    regulatory no regulatory clause found in the free agreements §3.3.2: comply with FDA and other regulators; "You agree that You will not seek any regulatory marketing permissions or make any determinations that may result in any Apple products being deemed regulated or that may impose any obligations or limitations on Apple"; market "only for its cleared or approved intended use"; provide clearance documents on request; notify Apple of regulatory complaints, after which "Apple may remove Your Application"
    privacy and health data Apple Developer Agreement §19 covers Apple's handling of the developer's own data §3.3.3: consent before collection; a privacy policy; protect data "from unauthorized use, disclosure or access"; notify users of a breach; HealthKit only "for health, motion, and/or fitness purposes" and never disclosed to third parties without express consent (§3.3.3(H)); not use Apple services "to create, receive, maintain or transmit any … 'protected health information'" or in a way that makes Apple a HIPAA "business associate"
    security of signing keys — (Xcode manages free-tier certificates) §5.1: "solely responsible for preventing any unauthorized person or organization from having access to Your Apple Certificates and keys"; "e.g., You will not upload Your Apple Certificate for App Store distribution to a cloud repository for use by a third party"; notify Apple of compromise "immediately"
    revocation "Apple reserves the right to revoke, disable or suspend any Provisioning Profiles … at any time, in its sole discretion" (Xcode §5); provisioning profiles revocable "e.g., if Apple suspects fraudulent or malicious activity" (§2.3) §5.4: Apple may revoke any certificate "at any time, in its sole discretion", with eight example grounds ending "(h) Apple has reason to believe that such action is prudent or necessary", and "may notify end users"; §5.3: notarization tickets revocable; "If Apple revokes Your Ticket or Your Apple Certificate, then Your Application may no longer run on macOS"
    termination rights end "automatically … without notice" on non-compliance (Xcode §5); Apple "may terminate or suspend you … at any time in Apple's sole discretion" (Apple Developer Agreement §10) one-year term renewing on payment (§11.1); termination on notice after a 30-day cure period, or at once for confidentiality breach, insolvency, sanctions or fraud (§11.2); "Either party may terminate this Agreement for its convenience … effective 30 days after providing the other party with written notice"
    export Apple Developer Agreement §16; Xcode §8.4 §14.8: no export to embargoed places or listed parties; on request, disclose "ultimate beneficial ownership, such as individuals with substantial control or 25% or greater ownership"; §5.3: no notarization uploads of software needing export authorisation
    fees none annual fee, "non-refundable", must be paid to submit (§8)
    changes Apple may modify the agreement "at its discretion" (Apple Developer Agreement §9) "Apple may change the Program Requirements or the terms of this Agreement at any time"; not accepting means suspension or termination (§4)
    publicity — no public statements about the agreement "without Apple's express prior written approval" (§9.4)

    What Apple takes on. From the same sources: reviewing submissions and, "if … selected by Apple", signing and distributing them (DPLA, Purpose); running notarization, with the caveat that its checks "should not be relied upon for malware detection or security verification of any kind" (§5.3); verifying and displaying EU trader details, a duty the DSA places on Apple [E5]; displaying a declared medical-device status [R7]; providing age signals [G1]; operating certificate issuance and revocation (§5.4; Apple's certificate policy at apple.com/certificateauthority); and, for In-App Purchase, payment processing and tax support [E4]. Apple's 2025 App Store Transparency Report gives the scale: 9,100,620 submissions reviewed, 2,093,244 rejected, 60,597,750 registered developers (all tiers), and 193,035 developer accounts terminated, 192,702 of them under "DPLA 3.2(f) — Fraud". The report cites export control as "DPLA 4.8"; in the current DPLA export control is §14.8 and §4 is "Changes to Program Requirements or Terms". The report's numbering may follow an older version; not verified.

    3. What Apple discloses about why¶

    Apple's FY2025 annual report (10-K filed 2025-10-31) and latest quarterly report (10-Q filed 2026-07-31) were read through a summarising fetch, not in full; quotations below should be re-checked against the filings.

    • [K1] Scrutiny. Under the heading "The technology industry, including, in some instances, the Company, is subject to intense media, political and regulatory scrutiny, which exposes the Company to increasing regulation, government investigations, legal actions and penalties": Apple "is the subject of investigations in Europe and other jurisdictions relating to App Store terms and conditions"; its DMA compliance plan "has been challenged by the Commission and may be challenged further by private litigants. The DMA provides for significant fines and penalties for noncompliance."
    • [K2] DMA. "On April 23, 2025, the Commission fined the Company €500 million in the Article 5(4) Investigation", with a cease-and-desist order to remove restrictions "that prevent developers from steering users to alternative distribution channels outside the App Store"; Apple has appealed. An Article 6(4) investigation was at preliminary findings; a final violation finding allows "fines up to 10% of the Company's annual worldwide net sales" (10-Q, Legal Proceedings).
    • [K3] Epic Games. On April 30, 2025 a district court found Apple in violation of a 2021 injunction. On December 11, 2025 the Ninth Circuit upheld the 2025 injunction in part and held "the Company can charge some commission on link-out purchases"; on June 30, 2026 the Supreme Court "granted the Company's petition to review the applicable legal standard for civil contempt" (10-Q).
    • [K4] Minors. Apple is "subject to new and changing laws, regulations and other legal obligations regarding online safety, including enhanced protections for minors and mandatory age verification requirements", which "can also result in significant penalties and fines, and legal liability" (10-Q, Risk Factors). Apple's own developer news on Texas SB2420 states that it is "concerned about the potential implications of laws like SB2420" (Nov 4, 2025).
    • [K5] Health. Under the product-defects risk factor: "These risks may increase as the Company's products are introduced into specialized applications, including health", and "the Company can be exposed to product liability claims" (10-K).
    • [K6] Commission, not fees. The 10-Q risk factors tie revenue risk to commission: if developers use alternative distribution and payment, "the Company may earn a lower commission on such sales, or may not earn a commission at all". Program-fee revenue was not found broken out in the passages read; whether any filing reports it is not verified.

    5. Testing the hypothesis¶

    The working hypothesis had five parts. The evidence for and against each:

    (a) Tiers of cost and commitment. Supported. Free (no fee, 7-day profiles, no distribution [T1][T5]), paid ($99, identity and entity checks [P1][P2]), enterprise ($299, 100 employees, interview, continuous evaluation [N1]), and add-ons that change the commercial terms (fee waiver [W1], Small Business Program [S1], EU terms [E1]). Commitment rises with each tier: legal identity, binding authority, then an organisation's internal controls.

    (b) Fees pay for insurance, review and compliance. Not supported by public evidence, and partly contradicted. - Apple does not say what the fee pays for. Program-fee revenue was not found in the filings read [K6], and the number of paying members is not published in the sources read (the 60.6 million "registered developers" includes free accounts). Whether the fee covers review cost cannot be computed from public sources. - The fee is the same for a game and a medical app, so it is not priced to risk. - Apple waives the fee for qualifying nonprofits, schools and governments that do not sell [W1]. A fee that funded review would be odd to waive for apps that still need review. - The commission is what Apple's filings treat as the revenue at stake [K6][K1]–[K3]. - On insurance, the contracts point the other way: Apple does not insure developers' risk; it caps its own liability at $50, disclaims life-critical use and takes an indemnity (§2). The developer carries the risk and any insurance for it. Whether Apple buys insurance for App Store activities is not disclosed in the sources read. - Counter-argument, fairly put: a fee plus D-U-N-S and identity checks is a cheap filter against fraud at scale, and fraud is by far the main ground for terminating accounts (192,702 of 193,035 in 2025). Read that way, the fee pays for accountability, by making each account cost something and tying it to a verified person or entity, rather than for review hours. This fits the evidence better than cost recovery, but Apple does not state it either.

    (c) Requirements are triggered by the characteristics of the assets and activities. Strongly supported. Health data triggers §3.3.3(H) and 5.1.3; medical claims trigger §3.3.2, 1.4.1, the clearance documents and the device-status declaration; dosing triggers 1.4.2; regulated fields trigger "legal entity, not individual"; selling triggers the Paid Applications Agreement, trader status and loss of the fee waiver; overriding silence triggers a managed entitlement; minors trigger age assurance; the EU triggers notarization and marketplace rules. The triggers are mostly declared by the developer (category, age questionnaire, trader self-assessment, device status), and Apple says plainly that some it cannot decide: "Apple can't determine whether you're a trader" [E5].

    (d) Device vendors will need comparable programmes, up and down. Consistent with the evidence, but Apple is not proof of it. Apple's program runs from governance (contract, entity checks, termination) down to technology (profile lifetimes, PPQ check-in, revocation). A device maker issuing credentials to third-party apps faces the same questions, set out in PARTNER-READINESS §7. The difference is that Apple's own regulatory position depends on staying out of device regulation (§3.3.2), while a pump or CGM manufacturer is the regulated party, so its program will carry duties (complaint handling, reporting) that Apple's does not.

    (e) The free tier as a model for data rights. Partly. - What fits: the tier exists for a person's "own personal, non-commercial use" [T5]; it needs no fee, no legal entity and no review; and the person is both developer and user, so no one else's accountability is engaged. - What does not fit: it is a licence, not a right. Apple may revoke it "at any time, in its sole discretion" and end it without notice (Xcode §2.3, §5; Apple Developer Agreement §10). It expires every 7 days by design [T1]. It excludes push notifications, NFC and managed capabilities such as Critical Alerts [T3][T4], which apps that read sensors or raise alarms commonly use. - Purpose of the time limit: not determinable from public sources. The accountability reading: short-lived, revocable trust for unverified identities, plus the agreement's fraud language (§2.3). The business reading: the limits make the paid program the practical route for anyone who needs reliability. Apple states neither purpose. One page suggests a looser use: the guidelines' introduction says an app "you just want to show to family and friends" can be installed "on a device for free", while the Xcode agreement limits free provisioning to devices "owned or controlled by You" (or by Authorized Developers for testing) and "Your own personal, non-commercial use". The two texts are not reconciled on Apple's pages.

    6.3 What the Apple model suggests for an ecosystem or vendor programme¶

    Interpretation, offered as questions for the discussion rather than recommendations:

    1. Price flat, scrutiny by trigger. Apple charges everyone the same and scales review by declared characteristics. A programme could ask each participant to declare the activities in §6.2 and attach the pillars to the declaration, rather than pricing or naming tiers by organisation type.
    2. A personal-use tier with a stated basis. Apple's free tier shows that a platform can carve out personal use with almost no organisational duty. For data rights, the difference that matters is whether that carve-out is a revocable licence (Apple's) or rests on a right and on agreement terms the person can enforce (PARTNER-READINESS §4: "revocation by process").
    3. Lifetimes chosen for safety, not only for control. Apple's lifetimes are 7 days (free), 90 days (TestFlight), one year (membership), and installed App Store apps keep working after membership ends [P4]. For a device that delivers insulin, expiry and revocation need a defined safe state, which Apple's model does not provide because it disclaims life-critical use.
    4. Keep the issuer out of the regulated role, or accept it. Apple's §3.3.2 forbids developers from making Apple "deemed regulated". A foundation sponsoring a programme faces the same choice that ECOSYSTEM-PROGRAMME §2b names: sponsor without operating, or operate with a named operator, contract and insurance.
    5. Legal entity where regulated. Guideline 5.1.1(ix) expects healthcare apps from "a legal entity that provides the services, and not … an individual developer". Any agreement with a manufacturer is likely to expect the same, which is the "who is accountable?" row of PARTNER-READINESS §3.
