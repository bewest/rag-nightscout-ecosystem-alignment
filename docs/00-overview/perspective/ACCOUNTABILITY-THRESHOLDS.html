    Accountability thresholds: the duties each activity brings¶

    Living document, last changed 2026-10-18 at 174c8e8e5.

    What this is: an analysis (part 2 of the overview, perspective; how the overview is organised), for the Nightscout Foundation board, the maintainers of the ecosystem projects, and the community. Read after precedents; leads to control structures. Living page, written 2026-10-16 against this repository at faafc57c as "Accountability as an organisation grows"; widened and retitled 2026-10-17 to cover every entity in the control structures, not only the foundation. It sets out which duties arrive with each asset an organisation holds and each activity it takes on, how mature programmes put those duties in place, and the conditions under which a US nonprofit takes on new legal, financial and insurance duties. Where it interprets, it says so. It makes no request. The evidence for §4 is in two research notes of 2026-10-17: Apple's developer program and vendor programmes and data rights.

    It is a draft that needs review by the foundation's accountant (§6), counsel and an insurance broker (§3, §4, §5) and, for anything touching insulin delivery, a regulatory professional, before anything rests on it. Thresholds and terms were checked on 2026-10-16 and 2026-10-17 against the publishers' pages or secondary summaries, as §9 lists; confirm each against the primary source and the jurisdictions that apply. It describes conditions in general and says nothing about what the foundation files, holds or has insured today; those are among the facts only the foundation can supply.

    1. The question¶

    A common prompt for an organisation to review its duties is the amount of money it handles. Money is one trigger among several, and for this ecosystem not the most important one. What an organisation must have in place follows from what it holds and what it does: reading someone's glucose data, hosting it for others, issuing a credential for a pump, commanding insulin delivery, signing with a manufacturer, taking money. This page lists those triggers and the duties each brings, for any entity in the proposed structure (the foundation, a hosting operator, a credential authority, a controller operator), so that the duties a proposal would bring can be seen before it is adopted rather than after.

    2. The pillars¶

    Interpretation. The duties group into nine pillars. A pillar is triggered by what an organisation does, not by what it is called. Eight are read from the way Apple's developer program apportions accountability (Apple note §6.1); the ninth, governance and oversight, is the nonprofit's own.

    pillar what it answers
    1. identity and standing who is accountable, and can they sign a contract
    2. allocation of liability who answers for harm, up to what, and with what insurance
    3. regulatory role which regulator sees whom as the manufacturer, for which intended use, under what quality system
    4. privacy and data protection consent, purpose, disclosure, retention, breach notice
    5. credentials and revocation who may act, for how long, and how access is withdrawn safely
    6. release verification what is checked before software reaches someone else
    7. money tax, filing, payments, consumer law, paying people
    8. continuity and exit what happens to the people already relying on it when the organisation stops
    9. governance and oversight board duties, conflicts of interest, a public record, and any rules the organisation sets for others

    4. How mature programmes put the pillars in place¶

    Facts as cited; the lessons are interpretation.

    • Apple prices flat and scales scrutiny by what the app does. The $99 annual fee is the same for every app and waived for nonprofits that sell nothing. What grows with an app's characteristics is review depth, approvals and declarations: medical apps get greater scrutiny and must link any clearance (guideline 1.4.1); dosage calculators must come from an approved entity or be approved (1.4.2); healthcare apps "should be submitted by a legal entity that provides the services, and not by an individual developer" (5.1.1(ix)); from 2026-03-26, health and medical apps in the US, EU and UK must declare whether they are a regulated medical device. The agreement caps Apple's liability at $50 and takes an indemnity from the developer. The fee does not visibly fund insurance; the identity checks and the fee make each account accountable, which fits Apple's report of 193,035 developer accounts terminated in 2025, 192,702 of them for fraud (Apple note §4–§5).
    • Apple's personal-use tier is a revocable licence. A free account can build for its own devices with 7-day profiles, 3 devices and 3 apps each, for "personal, non-commercial use". It carves personal use out of almost every organisational duty, but it rests on a licence Apple can withdraw, and its expiry has no defined safe state: Apple disclaims life-critical use.
    • Dexcom offers one agreement on the same terms to everyone, with tiers by scale. A registered developer can serve up to five people with data delayed one hour in the US and three hours elsewhere; beyond that comes "a standard, non-negotiable Data Licensing Agreement" and Dexcom's review; real-time access is by invitation (vendor note §1.2). No other pump or CGM maker publishes a developer programme.
    • The FDA's interoperable routes expect contracts. The decision summaries for the first ACE pump and iAGC describe the manufacturer's plan for sharing interface specifications as covering contracts and who handles complaints and adverse-event reporting (vendor note §2).

    Interpretation: across the three, identity comes before access, the price is the same for everyone, scrutiny scales with the activity, and liability moves to whoever acts. Credentials are issued for a stated time and can be revoked. For a device that delivers insulin, the programmes found do not supply what partner readiness §7 asks for: previously authorised, safely bounded operation that continues when a credential service is unreachable.

    5. The same requirements for anyone¶

    Interpretation: the author's view, tested against the evidence in §4 and in the vendor note §5.2.

    The requirements are the same for anyone who takes on an activity: in the US any organisation may meet them by fulfilling all of them, and in practice that means a legal entity with a quality system and contracts, usually a company. The FDA routes are open to any manufacturer that meets the special controls, and Tidepool, a nonprofit, took the same 510(k) route a company would (precedents). Dexcom's agreement is standard and non-negotiable. Washington's health-data act does not exclude nonprofits. Apple's only nonprofit concession is a fee waiver.

    A nonprofit therefore has no advantage in meeting the requirements of §3. Its role is a layer of oversight, at one level of governance, over the companies that build on a shared commons: CNCF certifies vendors' offerings against one upstream with the same public tests, and the Open Home Foundation binds its commercial partners by contract, without either carrying those companies' own regulatory duties. That role is pillar 9, and it answers the community's need for a party that is not one of the competitors.

    On the engineering side, the cost is falling for everyone: coding agents are used for increasingly ambitious projects, device connectivity among them (the author's observation; no source read measures it). That lowers the cost of building an integration. It does not change who is authorised to use one: device authentication, app attestation and issued credentials are met by an entity and its agreements, which is the structure drawn in 1·proposed, 2·agreement and 2·controller-operator.

    6. A US nonprofit's own thresholds¶

    The money and staffing conditions that bring the foundation itself new filing, tax and employment duties, whatever activities it takes on.

    6.1 Money¶

    condition what it brings
    gross receipts normally above $50,000 the annual return moves from the Form 990-N e-Postcard to Form 990-EZ
    gross receipts of $200,000 or more, or total assets of $500,000 or more the full Form 990. Its Part VI asks whether the organisation has a written conflict-of-interest policy, a whistleblower policy and a document-retention policy, whether the board reviewed the return, and whether it keeps minutes of board and committee meetings at the time they happen
    asking for donations from residents of a state that requires registration charitable-solicitation registration and annual reports in that state (Colorado is one)
    revenue above a state's audit level, where registered financial statements reviewed or audited by an independent accountant: in New York, a review between $250,000 and $1 million in revenue and an audit above $1 million; in California, an audit at $2 million
    $1 million or more of federal awards spent in a fiscal year a Single Audit under 2 CFR 200 Subpart F (the threshold rose from $750,000 for fiscal years beginning on or after 2024-10-01)

    Interpretation: at six figures, the money triggers are mostly about filing and disclosure. The return becomes the public record of the governance practices in Part VI.

    6.2 Paying people¶

    condition what it brings
    paying anyone for work deciding whether each person is a contractor or an employee, which follows how the work is controlled, not what the agreement calls it
    paying someone with influence over the organisation (a director, officer, founder or their family) the excess-benefit rules (Internal Revenue Code §4958). Pay set by directors without a conflict, using comparable pay data, and recorded at the time, is presumed reasonable (26 CFR 53.4958-6); without that, excess pay can bring penalty taxes on the person and on the managers who approved it
    the first employee payroll taxes and filings; workers' compensation in most states (in Texas it is optional for private employers; it depends on the state where the person works); employment-practices insurance becomes relevant
    paying through a grant made to a project or a third party a written grant agreement, and records showing the money was used for the exempt purpose

    7. Where the proposals meet these conditions¶

    Interpretation. Each proposal in part 3, and each entity in the proposed structure, would bring some of the conditions above if adopted:

    proposal or entity conditions it would bring
    sponsored maintenance team paying people (§6.2), possibly including people with influence over the foundation; employee or contractor status
    datalake pilot hosting or processing health data; research with identifiable data
    partner readiness a manufacturer agreement; complaint records that carry health data
    operating a shared service operating a service that people depend on; this is why the programme proposes sponsoring by default
    collaboration rules money from companies that also seek something; disclosure; overseeing companies that build on the commons
    a hosting operator (1·proposed) hosting health data, operating a service, taking money
    a credential authority operator (2·agreement) holding or issuing credentials, operating a service, a manufacturer agreement
    a regulated controller operator (2·controller-operator) commanding insulin delivery, distributing software, a manufacturer agreement, hosting health data

    The infrastructure-transition frame asks when the work of keeping software current stops being something to leave to whoever has time. This page asks the matching question for the organisations: which duties arrive with each thing they take on.

    8. What this frame does not show¶

    • It is not legal, tax, insurance or regulatory advice. It lists conditions to raise with professionals.
    • Law varies by place. Registration, audit levels, workers' compensation, privacy law and device regulation depend on the states and countries involved.
    • It does not show which conditions apply now. That depends on facts the foundation and each operator hold.
    • Insurance terms vary by policy. The cover named in §3 is what is usually considered, not what any policy includes.
    • Apple's and Dexcom's reasons are inferred. §4 reads mechanisms from public terms; what either company intends, or what its fees pay for, is not in the sources read.
