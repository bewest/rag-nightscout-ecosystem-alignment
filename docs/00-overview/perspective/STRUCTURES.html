    Control structures, drawn¶

    Living document, last changed 2026-10-16 at 3733ea3c.

    For the Nightscout Foundation board, the maintainers of the ecosystem projects, and the community. Perspective (part 2 of the overview; how the overview is organised). Living page, written 2026-10-16. Drawings to follow and discuss the overview's proposals with: who controls whom, what comes back, and where money flows. Every node and edge comes from a page linked under the drawing; nothing here is a new finding. The drawings marked proposed show a proposal for discussion, not a decision.

    Author's interests: the author maintains cgm-remote-monitor, founded a company that hosts Nightscout commercially, and could be a candidate for paid work under the related proposals. The collaboration rules §5 would apply to that company and to the author like anyone else. Operators in the drawings are roles, not named companies.

    How to read the drawings¶

    The drawings follow the convention of STPA (system-theoretic process analysis) control structures. Whoever controls sits above what they control. Control actions run down the left of each loop and the feedback that tells a controller what happened runs up the right. A loop with no feedback edge is a loop whose controller cannot see the result of what it does.

    Every drawing is labelled by zoom level, so a box in one drawing can be opened up in another:

    label zoom level what it shows
    Z0 sector governments, regulators, companies, clinicians and the commons around AID in general
    Z1 ecosystem the Nightscout ecosystem's parts, today and as proposed, with one view per stakeholder
    Z2 one agreement what one agreement with one manufacturer has to carry
    Z2b one regulated controller Loop-as-a-service: an entity that answers to a regulator for a controller built from an open-source codebase
    Z3 one person's loop the controller, pump, sensor and body, and where an issued credential touches them
    P1–P3 precedents SQLite, CNCF and the Apple Developer Program, each mapped onto Z1 or Z2

    A box marked [zoom: Z2] is drawn in more detail in Z2. Markers are used only where the link between two drawings would not be obvious otherwise: Z0's commons is the whole of Z1; Z1's credential authority is where the agreements of Z2 reach the projects; Z1's regulated controller operator is Z2b; Z1's devices and Z2's device line and Z2b's controller app are the loop drawn in Z3. The levels are views of one system, not separate systems, so the same box can appear at several levels: the credential authority is in Z1, Z2 and Z3.

    Z0 · Sector¶

    The two columns follow Leveson's sociotechnical form: development on the left, operations on the right. The commons sits between them because it is both: specifications and release processes on one side, documentation and hubs that people use on the other.

    Z1 · Ecosystem, today¶

    Sources: how decisions are made today §1, §3 and §4; partner readiness §1 and §3. What the drawing shows by its gaps:

    • No edge joins a project or the foundation to a manufacturer. Projects reach devices through protocols volunteers worked out (a data edge, not an agreement).
    • No complaint or incident route leaves the ecosystem. Feedback from people reaches projects as issues and logs, and stops there.
    • The only funding edges are the foundation's support to projects and subscriptions to hosted providers.

    Z1 · Ecosystem, proposed¶

    Sources: ecosystem programme §2b (the foundation sponsors by default and operates only by recorded decision); partner readiness §2 (tiers A, B and C), §6 (complaint intake) and §7 (registry, credential authority and hosting kept apart); precedents (Tidepool Loop: a regulated track beside the DIY one).

    What changes against today: each new edge to a manufacturer runs through a named legal entity. The foundation signs for tier A, observe and export. Tier B and C relationships go to an operator with its own contract, insurance and successor: a credential authority, or a regulated controller operator as Tidepool was for Loop. Complaints gain a route back to the manufacturer and the regulator. The DIY track keeps its own edges to people. The manufacturer is drawn above the foundation because in this drawing it grants access; that is the direction of control, not a ranking.

    The same drawing, from each stakeholder's side¶

    Each view greys out everything that does not touch the stakeholder. The layout does not change, so the views can be laid side by side.

    view what this stakeholder controls what reaches them as feedback
    a person with diabetes or a caregiver which releases, hosted hubs and devices they use, and whether to pay a host glucose and alarms from devices; nothing returns from the complaints they send unless the intake replies
    a project maintainer releases to people conformance results go up; credentials and tests come down from bodies the project does not run
    a Nightscout-as-a-service operator the hosted hubs subscriptions; hub conformance is proposed, not existing
    a manufacturer agreements, credential authority, clearance submissions a readiness dossier, forwarded complaints and field failures, none of which exist today
    the foundation board sponsorship of shared operations the tier A agreement and nothing else; it operates nothing in this drawing
    a regulator clearance of manufacturers and of any regulated controller operator adverse events and submissions; the DIY track has no edge to a regulator

    The "feedback" column is read from the drawing; it is where the stakeholders are likely to disagree, and where the drawing should be corrected if it is wrong.

    Z2 · One agreement¶

    Source: partner readiness §4 (terms that carry data rights), §6 (complaints in both directions) and §7 (three roles kept apart; separating request from effect). The registry says who qualifies, the credential authority issues credentials, and the client release is hosted or built by someone else again. The client's commands to the device are requests; what comes back is the state the device applied, the split the hub-sync architecture already makes.

    Z2b · One regulated controller: Loop-as-a-service¶

    A proposal for discussion. Sources: the special controls for interoperable automated glycemic controllers (iAGC, 21 CFR 862.1356), integrated CGMs (iCGM, 862.1355) and alternate controller enabled pumps (ACE, 880.5730), as summarised in the stack census cloud layer; history and precedents for Tidepool Loop, cleared as an iAGC on 2023-01-23 (K203689); partner readiness §2 (tier C) and §7.

    What makes this a different drawing from Z2:

    • The operator is the legal manufacturer of the controller. It answers to the regulator for one validated release and its updates. The DIY track continues beside it, as DIY Loop did beside Tidepool Loop.
    • One codebase. The cleared release is a validated build of the same open-source releases, not a separate edition (programme, "One codebase, two uses": SQLite sells assurance on the releases free users get; CNCF certifies vendors against one upstream). Fixes and validation evidence from the regulated track return to the shared codebase, drawn as a proposed edge. The reason for the edge: without it the regulated track becomes a second upstream, and the open project and the operator compete for the same fixes and the same maintainers' time.
    • The interfaces are regulated on both sides. The iAGC controls ask for validated interface specifications, secure authentication (pairing) and a process for sharing and validating the interface with connected devices. The ACE pump and iCGM controls ask the device side for the same. The pump and CGM manufacturers' edges to the operator are therefore agreements, not protocols worked out by volunteers.
    • The controller's inputs and outputs are restricted. Under the iAGC regulation it takes glucose only from iCGMs and commands only ACE pumps, unless the regulator determines otherwise. An ACE pump must fall back safely when it loses the connection.
    • Feedback has a record. The regulation asks for a record of critical events. Together with complaints, it returns to the operator and from there to the regulator, which the DIY loop in Z1-today does not have.

    The drawing does not settle who operates such an entity, whether the foundation sponsors it (programme §2b: sponsoring does not make the foundation the operator), or how the return edge works when a fix to the open codebase has not yet been validated for the cleared release. The regulatory summary above paraphrases the regulations; it is for regulatory counsel to confirm against the current text and any device-specific clearance.

    Z3 · One person's loop¶

    Source: partner readiness §7, "no single point of failure for therapy". The credential authority is the only node above the person that can reach the loop, and the drawing puts it outside the loop: the app holds a grant that stays valid offline within bounds, so the authority being unreachable does not stop the loop. What the bounds are is for each manufacturer to define and validate; the drawing does not describe how any released system behaves.

    Precedents¶

    P1 SQLite Z1, foundation and shared operations companies buy assurance through one small company; no edge runs from a buyer to the developers
    P2 CNCF Z1 and Z2, conformance and registry many vendors certified against one upstream by the same public tests
    P3 Apple Developer Program Z2 and Z3, credential authority a platform owner enrols developers, issues signing credentials that expire, and approves special entitlements

    Source: precedents, SQLite, read from sqlite.org 2026-09-29.

    Source: precedents, funding and rules rows (charter, TOC process, conformance and KCSP pages, read 2026-09-29 and 2026-10-01).

    Source: TestFlight distribution infrastructure §1, §3 and §4. Self-built apps put one developer account, and its yearly certificate, on every person who builds; App Store apps put one account on the publisher. That is the trade a credential authority in Z2 makes too: who holds the credential decides who answers for it and whose renewal can lapse.

    What the drawings do not show¶

    • Legal relationships in detail. An edge labelled "agreement" stands for terms that partner readiness §4–§5 lists; which party is the manufacturer for which function, liability and intellectual property are for counsel and are not drawn.
    • Sizes and amounts. Edges show that money flows, not how much.
    • Every participant. Clinicians, the support community and other hubs appear in Z0 only.

    Changing a drawing¶

    The drawings are generated. Each one's source is a YAML file in tools/structure-diagrams/inputs/, with nodes placed by level and column, edges typed as control, feedback, funding or data, and optional stakeholder views. Edit the YAML and run python3 tools/structure-diagrams/render.py. Graphviz was chosen because it was the one tool that kept hierarchy, columns and the control-left convention on every structure in the diagram tool lab.
