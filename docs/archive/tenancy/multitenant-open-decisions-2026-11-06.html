    Multitenant: the five decisions on the critical path (2026-11-06)¶

    Record

    Record as of 2026-11-06. A point-in-time document; it is not kept up to date.

    Retained record, archived 2026-11-10 from alignment c53105eeeb04225910e3c6871629fed5c7b78853. The captured text below preserves its five critical-path questions as asked. Each item has a recorded decision or deferral in release decisions, and the M5/M6 decision list is the current index. Do not act on its “next” or open questions.

    What this is: a decision note for the maintainer (contributor-facing), written 2026-11-06 against alignment main 2efaa3a89 and the published candidate 83582e87 on draft PR #8898 (the owner/evaluator candidate 4cd95d06 is in its comprehensive campaign). Each item names the mechanism, the trade-off, what operators or clients would notice, what is measured and what is only read, and whether it is single- or multitenant. One topic per section; recommendations are given because they were asked for.

    1. Selected owner mode: what a root credential gets¶

    Mechanism (read-derived). In the optional selected-reader mode the data listener resolves every operation through the current-authority reader (lib/tenant-authorization/access-postgres.js:18, :51-59): the caller must map to a subject session, credential and grants in one SQL snapshot. A legacy root carrier (an api-secret header, which is all pinned cgmsim can send) has no subject row; the reader's sanitized failure path reports ACCESS_AUTHORITY_UNAVAILABLE, which the HTTP layer renders as 503 (access-postgres.js:36, :59).

    Measured (reproduced, R63 on 87133959). oref0's three subject-token upload workflows match the unselected reference in response summary, exit and stored values; an emptied ceiling gives 401 with no effect on the next upload; cgmsim's root-secret upload gets 503 with no effect, where the unselected reference gives 200 and a write. Only summaries and normalized stored values were compared; raw reply-body parity is unmeasured.

    Trade-off. 503 says "the service is unavailable, retry", which is false and makes retrying clients hammer a healthy site; 401 says "not authorised", which is true and stable, but a client that treats 401 as a configuration error may stop uploading. Admitting the root carrier under selected mode would reintroduce an all-domain credential inside the mode whose purpose is a bounded catalogue.

    Who notices. Multitenant only; nothing changes for single-tenant or for the unselected default. An operator who selects the mode on a tenant whose uploader is cgmsim (or any root-carrier client) sees that uploader fail until it can present a subject token.

    Options. (a) Map "no subject for this carrier" to 401 with no effect, keep activation off; (b) keep 503 as observed; (c) admit root carriers in selected mode with the tenant's full ceiling; (d) decide nothing until clients can carry subject tokens.

    Recommendation: (a). Correct status, no retry storm, no widening of the catalogue; it costs one mapping in the reader plus a re-run of the selected-client lab with the predicted 401. Activation stays off until (d)'s client work lands, which is a client-side change, not a Nightscout one.

    2. What M2 is held to: the roadmap cell or the dashboard's additions¶

    Mechanism (read-derived, promotion review of 2026-11-06). The roadmap's M2 cell lists five properties: tenant-local effective config and versioned writes; unknown tenant refuses; a token for A fails on B by signature; no environment, root or key leakage; separate owner, data and operator routes. The dashboard's T30-WIRING and T30-TENANT-ADMIN rows add four more: human issuer and refresh, existing-grant clamping, tenant lifecycle (provision, suspend, export, delete) and production trust for resolution. The roadmap itself places issuer/refresh and delegation ceilings under M6.

    Measured. Of the roadmap's five: four are measured (R33/R34 unknown-tenant refusal, R48 signature refusal, R51 tenant-local config, R62 current grants); versioned writes are on the integrated head but the slice 011802ba is still marked "testing pending"; route separation now has an actual two-listener run on 4cd95d06 (owner native 9/0/0: DATA management 405, OWNER 200). Of the dashboard's four: all unmeasured, three recorded as unimplemented (R53, R54); the lifecycle packet is being drafted by session -8c.

    Trade-off. Holding M2 to the roadmap cell lets M2 promote once 011802ba is re-measured on the final head and the two-listener run is in a comprehensive campaign; M3 and M4 then proceed on operator-provisioned credentials (as R48 already does). Holding M2 to the dashboard list parks M2, M3, M4 and therefore M6 behind human-identity work that the roadmap already assigned to M6.

    Who notices. Multitenant only. Operators of the preview provision owner credentials by hand either way until M6; the difference is only when the records may say "M2 promoted".

    Options. (a) Roadmap cell is the M2 bar; the four additions are re-homed to M6 in the dashboard and the status overlay; (b) the dashboard list is the bar; (c) split: clamping and production trust stay in M2, issuer and lifecycle go to M6.

    Recommendation: (a). It is what the roadmap says, it keeps the dependency chain honest (M2 was never the identity milestone), and the lifecycle packet gives M6 its criteria. Clamping of existing grants is already partly measured by R62's current-ceiling enforcement and can be listed under M6 with the rest.

    3. Numeric bounds and performance budgets¶

    Mechanism. The only numeric limit in product code is the PostgreSQL sort-key cap, MAX_SORT_KEYS = 5 at lib/storage/postgres-filter.js:34 (raised from 4 in R51; over the cap the query is refused as unsupported, :216). Every other bound the roadmap names (write latency, throughput, replay retention horizon, per-tenant evaluator cost, import resource use) has no number in code and no approved threshold.

    Measured. R65 (descriptive, four cohorts of 40 HTTP 201 requests, no errors): p95 18.2–23.1 ms, 303–339 successful requests/s, with the commit clock on or off. The clock benchmark is opt-in and has been pending in every corpus run; R51 says sort-cost performance is unmeasured; the durable contract's section 9 lists retention horizon and budgets as UNMEASURED.

    Trade-off. Approving thresholds now from four short runs would turn descriptive numbers into pass gates with no steady-state or contention basis, and a wrong threshold either blocks promotion on noise or waves through a regression. Deferring them leaves M1's "resource bounds" and M6's "bounded resources" criteria open until a controlled window is run.

    Who notices. Multitenant only; single-tenant Mongo is unaffected. The sort cap is the one bound a client can hit today: a v3 query sorting on more than five keys is refused on PG and accepted on Mongo.

    Options. (a) Approve a threshold table now; (b) declare bounds deferred to batch C, let M1 promote with "resource bounds: deferred, measured in C" stated, and commission one controlled benchmark window (benchmark flag on, quiet machine, steady state with the independent stored-count oracle R65 lacked); (c) refuse M1 until bounds are approved.

    Recommendation: (b). Thresholds should come from a measurement designed to set them, not from the first numbers available. The sort cap stays at 5 and is recorded as an intentional PG-only difference in the compatibility page.

    4. What "actual client execution" means for M4¶

    Mechanism (read-derived, M4 packet). Pinned clients fall into feasibility classes: A runs headless on Linux (cgmsim, oref0, nightscout-connect); B needs a vendor-cloud stand-in that does not exist here (librelink-up); C is a JVM module inside an Android project (AAPS NSClientV3 SDK, build cost unmeasured); D is an Android app (xDrip+, AAPS) needing an emulator; E is iOS/watchOS (Loop, Trio, LoopFollow, LoopCaregiver, nightguard, xDrip4iOS, DiaBLE) needing an Apple toolchain. The roadmap's M4 cell asks for v1 and v3 uploader/follower shapes "exercised on pinned versions" and distinguishes actual execution from source-pattern replay.

    Measured. Class A: 82/82 checks, 19 paired cells, 0 differences on 87133959 (R62) and on d89a44e1 (R60). Classes B–E: source-pattern replay only (R40, two 768-cell corpora), plus the pinned AAPS 3.4.2.3 v1 socket sequence as a driven replay. No mobile binary has been executed.

    Trade-off. Promoting M4 on class A plus replay gives every mobile client only read-derived compatibility evidence; iOS clients are the majority of real uploaders and followers. An emulator lab (class D) is buildable on Linux at a cost nobody has measured; class E needs Apple hardware or CI and a human.

    Who notices. Multitenant preview operators whose users run iOS or Android clients: a difference in retry, batch or refusal handling would surface in the field first. Single-tenant is unaffected; the v1/v3 surface is unchanged there.

    Options. (a) Promote M4 on class A execution plus replay for B–E, with the "no all-client claim" line in the record; (b) add a class D emulator lab (xDrip+, AAPS) as a batch C item before M6, not before M4; (c) require class D before M4; (d) require D and E before M4.

    Recommendation: (a) now, (b) in C. M4 gates M6 through the chain, and the headless clients already exercise every v1 and v3 shape the roadmap names. The emulator lab is worth funding because it converts the largest read-derived block into measurement, but it should not hold the chain.

    5. Who owns the rest of batch C¶

    Mechanism. The batching plan's section 5 now assigns two of the formerly unassigned items to session -8c: the tenant export/import fidelity and deletion-with-recoverable-keys proof (lane codex/tenant-export-delete-20261106 off 83582e87), and the owner session/issuer lifecycle decision packet. Still unassigned: backup/restore on the assembled tree, upgrade/rollback, resource and egress bounds, and the final-tree review. The plan says C must prepare tools and populated fixtures in parallel with A and B, and that no one may claim C implementation complete while an item is unassigned.

    Measured. R62's populated PostgreSQL dump/restore lab (stopped-write synthetic reference with explicit ACL recreation) is the only C-stage instrument that has run. Nothing else in C has a lane, a worktree or a number.

    Trade-off. Assigning now costs a worktree off 83582e87 and a source-only lane until a focused run; waiting until A freezes keeps C serial behind A and B, which the plan was written to avoid.

    Who notices. Multitenant only. Operators of the preview have no backup, upgrade or rollback procedure until this lands; it is the M6 gate most visible to someone running the preview for real.

    Options. (a) Open one new lane now for backup/restore and upgrade/rollback (they depend only on the migration runner and R62's populated lab); bounds and final-tree review follow item 3's benchmark window and the frozen C candidate; (b) hand the remainder to -8c after export/delete lands; (c) hand it to the Codex root coordinator after the A freeze.

    Recommendation: (a). It is the one C item that can start today without a decision pending, and it is the one an operator would ask for first.
