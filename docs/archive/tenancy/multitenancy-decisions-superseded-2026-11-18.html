    From: Plan restructuring and process simplification (2026-11-09)¶

    decision who, when scope as it stands
    The deployment tree (deploy/multitenant: Compose, nginx, bootstrap, restore, UI) lives in the sibling repository nightscout-deploy. Core keeps application code, the Dockerfile and a pointer. The move is not gated on re-running acceptance; the ed2 evidence keeps its attribution because the files are relocated, not changed. maintainer, 2026-11-09 (confirms item 1 of the deploy-companion note) Deployment packaging, edge, identity-provider profiles, secrets sidecars, operator CLI Imported with history on 2026-11-09 (bindings/compose/multitenant, byte-identity check against ed2). Deployment-trust work items carry repo: nightscout-deploy in the queue. Core's copy is removed when the Compose binding renders from a plan (deploy roadmap wave 2).
    Evaluator scaling: a feature flag with two environment variables, the member count and this worker's ordinal, partitions tenants by hash onto a fixed slice; each evaluator works only its slice. Flag off means one evaluator owns every tenant, as today. Operator-configured, in the Consul bootstrap-expect style; a StatefulSet supplies the ordinal, the renderer sets the count. Exclusivity comes from a pool-membership table, not a per-tenant lock. maintainer, 2026-11-09 (discussion of ring/ordinal partitioning; per-tenant lease rejected as unnecessary overhead) Evaluator role, Compose and Kubernetes bindings; M7 MT-EVALUATOR-PARTITION Each member keeps one heartbeat row: ordinal, expected member count, assigned ring slice, lease expiry. Before each cycle a member reads the table; it evaluates its slice only while every live row agrees on the member count and no two live rows share an ordinal, and otherwise stops its own cycle and raises an alert. Cost is one row update per member per heartbeat instead of one claim per tenant per cycle. Duplicate evaluation is then bounded to one heartbeat interval after a membership change, and the evaluator's outputs are idempotent by contract (computed view keyed per tenant; notification intent with stable identity and suppression), so a brief overlap produces no duplicate clinical effect. A per-tenant commit fence is not required; if MT-SCALE-UNBOUND measures a duplicate external effect in the overlap window, that measurement reopens the question. Plain modulo is adequate; consistent hashing is an optimisation to measure.

    Multitenant cost scales with activity, not tenant count: work is proportional to writes, viewers × changes and due timers, and an idle tenant costs close to nothing. The evaluator, realtime and restore-fence paths are changed in model, not tuned: (1) evaluator work on change, with a per-member aggregate change query on an interval (the D6 backstop shape) and per-tenant due-time timers for time-driven alarms, replacing the per-tenant one-second poll; (2) realtime publishes on change, authorization checked at connect and invalidated by events (expiry, revocation, pause, suspend), and the initial frame queued under pool pressure instead of a server disconnect; (3) the restore fence stays database-enforced but is checked by a cheap per-tenant epoch or shared lock instead of a catalogue query per transaction; plus bounded startup warm and pool sizes from configuration.
