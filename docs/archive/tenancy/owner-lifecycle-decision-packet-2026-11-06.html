    2. The questions, one topic each, in dependency order¶

    Dependency order: Q3 (who mints, which audience) is needed by Q2 (what a session is) and Q1 (who the person is); Q4 and Q5 sit on top. They are presented Q1 → Q5 as briefed, with the dependency named in each.

    Q1. Human identity provider for owners¶

    Later direction adopted: shared optional OIDC integration serves equivalent single/multitenant human authentication through separate authority adapters. No provider-specific deployment or callback/cookie contract is selected by that direction. The source survey and alternatives below retain their scope.

    Mechanism. D17 row 2 as adopted in principle (2026-10-12): Kratos, one cohort-wide pool, cookie scope E, four production conditions, no real identities until the compliance question has an owner. D17 as amended (2026-10-27): the provider is interchangeable ("bring your own auth": Kratos, Clerk or another), behind Nightscout's own token exchange, "to keep in-house human-identity security surface small"; owed: the adapter interface and the provider properties a hoster must supply. The owner authority contract (owner-authority.md:82-87): "Human authentication uses an interchangeable identity provider; Nightscout maps verified provider/subject to current tenant access and exchanges it for a tenant-bound management token. No custom password/recovery system. Provider sessions alone do not certify current Nightscout grants." The schema spec settles the membership key as (provider, subject_id), text, no foreign key, per tenant (Q10, Q19; table renamed tenant_access_grants, Q18).

    What is measured. T30-ORY-PROOF (2026-10-07, Kratos v1.3.1/Hydra v2.3.0 and v26.2.0): 23/23 on both lines; isolation is held entirely by the nsjwt exchange (Host-first resolution, membership row, per-tenant key, client-id check), not by Ory; a minted token lives to its own exp (3600 s in the lab) whatever happens to the Kratos session (§2 revocation rows). Cookie variants (2026-10-12): E 21/21; H2 24/26 with the two misses being cross-origin writes once a tenant session exists, and a Kratos logout not reaching tenant sessions (§0 items 2-4). Neither ran over https, through socket.io, or against Nightscout's real API (§6). Read, not run: every claim about Clerk or any provider other than Kratos; the adapter interface; NRG's pipeline on current Ory beyond §5 of the proof.

    Options.

    • (a) Generic OIDC adapter behind the exchange; Kratos is the measured reference deployment. Nightscout implements one adapter contract (issuer, audience, subject claim, session-id claim, logout back-channel if offered), and ships Kratos's configuration as the reference because E's edge conditions were measured for it. Owners sign in at the provider; ns-tenant-admin never sees a password.
    • (b) Kratos only, as D17 row 2 before the amendment: one pool, scope E, and Kratos's session API as the session of record.
    • (c) Native owner password in Nightscout (hashing, recovery, MFA in lib/), no provider.
    • (d) Defer: management credentials stay operator-provisioned (the R48 shape) until a provider is chosen; owners never log in during the preview.

    Trade-offs by axis.

    axis (a) generic adapter, Kratos reference (b) Kratos only (c) native password (d) defer
    long-term direction matches D17 as amended; the exchange is the seam the proof showed carries isolation contradicts the amendment; a cohort pool is "effectively irreversible once identities exist" (§2.9) contradicts owner-authority.md:85 "No custom password/recovery system" leaves M6's human-identity criterion without an implementation path
    architecture one adapter interface; provider state stays outside the tenant tables provider-specific session API in the exchange password store inside the tenant DB; recovery, MFA, lockout all ours unchanged
    backward / forward compat single unchanged; provider swap is configuration a hoster with Clerk or an enterprise IdP cannot use it none for single; every future provider needs a second path none
    maintenance adapter surface small; provider updates are the hoster's we track Kratos releases (calendar versions, §0 item 6) highest: password storage and recovery "need updates in the wild" (amendment) none now, all later
    reliability provider outage: existing sessions continue through our session row (Q2); new logins refuse (contract :141-143) same, plus Kratos's cohort-wide session scope hazards (§4.1) no external dependency; but recovery flows are the usual breach surface n/a
    performance one exchange per login; per-operation cost is ours either way (R48) same same same

    Who notices. Owners: under (a)/(b) they sign in at a provider-hosted page and land on their tenant; under (c) at a Nightscout page; under (d) they cannot sign in at all. Operators: (a)/(b) require the edge conditions of scope E (strip Kratos and CSRF cookies before any tenant backend, refuse cross-origin writes including WebSocket upgrades, every path through the edge, measured over https) or an equivalent for their provider; (c) requires none, and gives them a password database to protect. Multitenant only. single keeps API_SECRET.

    Recommendation: (a). Direction: it is D17 as amended, and the proof already showed the exchange, not the provider, carries isolation. Architecture: the adapter boundary is the only place provider differences live. Compatibility: single untouched; a hoster swaps providers by configuration. Maintenance: the smallest in-house surface that still works. Reliability: the session of record is ours (Q2), so a provider outage degrades to "no new logins", which the contract already specifies. Performance: unchanged from R48. The compliance-owner condition in D17 row 2 is unchanged by this choice and remains a maintainer gate before any real identity exists. Unmeasured: any provider other than Kratos; the adapter against Nightscout's actual listener; E's edge over https and through socket.io.

    Q2. Session model and lifetimes¶

    Renewal direction adopted: provider-backed renewal first, with existing Nightscout current authority and adopted limits. This supersedes the earlier recommendation (a) as the initial implementation selection. Nightscout-owned rotating refresh is not an initial requirement. Logout/idle/outage/concurrency and transport contracts still need acceptance; none of the numeric proposals below is approved by this direction.

    Mechanism. The contract's table row (owner-authority.md:27): "Refresh/session credential: PostgreSQL session record and one-way verifier; rotation/reuse detection or provider-supported session equivalent." Adopted initial limits (:97-102): human management access token 10 min; session idle / absolute 30 min / 12 h; freshness for reveal, ownership changes and privileged credential issuance within 5 min; existing device/client exchange JWT 8 h preserved. "Idle/absolute expiry requires current server-side session enforcement. Token expiry is not revocation" (:105-106). In code: access_sessions has expires_at (absolute) and active, nothing else about time; the verifier refuses a token whose exp exceeds the session's expires_at (access-token.js:68) and refuses when the session is inactive or its generation lags the credential's (access-postgres.js:79). The serving reader is SELECT-only by contract and startup screen ("must not own tables, bypass RLS or mutate authority", access-tokens.md:33-36; access-postgres.js:27-36). The lab token was 3600 s (ORY proof §2); the preview data token is 28800 s (preview-config.js:160).

    Measured. R48: committed session revocation refuses the same token on the next read across independent processes (controls: removing the session-active check turns a revoked session's GET from 403 to 200). The November 5 measurement: 16 semantic checks, 4 controls, 2880 timed operations; local arms make 0 Vault calls per operation. Read: everything about refresh, rotation, reuse detection and idle expiry; none of it exists in code.

    Options.

    • (a) PostgreSQL session row is the session of record, extended with a refresh verifier (one-way hash of a random refresh secret), refresh rotation state, an idle deadline and a rotation family id. Only the issuer writes authority state (Q3). Every refresh rotates the secret; reuse handling still needs the separately proposed retry/grace contract. Each reader remains SELECT-only and checks the current idle deadline, absolute expiry, active state and generation on every protected operation; the issuer checks both deadlines before refresh. Checking idle only at refresh would let an already-issued token operate after idle expiry.

    Proposed, not adopted or implemented: qualifying human activity is an authenticated, explicit owner interaction delivered to the issuer for a current session. The issuer alone advances that session's idle deadline after validating the interaction and current authority. Automatic refresh, background polling and receipt of stream traffic do not extend human activity. The activity-reporting route, ordering relative to a protected operation, cross-worker propagation and treatment of activity arriving at/after the deadline require a bounded contract and measurement. No asynchronous stale-acceptance window is adopted by this proposal. - (b) Provider-held sessions: no refresh of our own; the browser re-exchanges the provider session for a new 10-minute token; our access_sessions row mirrors the provider's session id. - (c) Reader-written activity tracking: each operation updates last_used_at in the readers. This is distinct from (a)'s SELECT-only per-operation idle check. - (d) Long-lived management token (hours) with no refresh, revoked only by session row.

    Trade-offs by axis.

    axis (a) PG session + rotating refresh (b) provider sessions (c) reader-written activity (d) long token
    direction the contract's first-named form; "provider sessions alone do not certify current grants" (:85-86) the contract's "provider-supported equivalent", conditional on logout propagation, which H2 measured as absent (intact.logout) contradicts the read-only reader; R48 measured the reader with no mutation grant contradicts the 10-minute limit
    architecture one writer (issuer), many readers; matches R48's split exchange depends on provider session API per adapter turns every data worker into an authority writer simplest; weakest
    compat single unchanged; device tokens untouched (8 h preserved) tied to provider same as (a) for clients none
    maintenance our table, our rules; one more migration in the access candidate per-provider session semantics lock contention paths to characterise least
    reliability provider outage: refresh continues until idle/absolute expire (contract :141 "Provider/identity outages ... need their own explicit acceptance") provider outage ends all management sessions at the next 10-minute boundary write on the hot path under outage long exposure window of a stolen token
    performance refresh writes plus issuer-only qualifying-activity writes; load unmeasured one exchange per 10 min per owner one UPDATE per operation (R48 measured reads at 2.8/2.3 ms p50 combined; a write per op is unmeasured) none

    Who notices (proposed). Owners under (a) may refresh silently while the current idle and absolute deadlines permit it; automatic refresh must not keep an unattended session alive. The adopted human lifetime limits still need implementation and acceptance. Provider-held sessions under (b) need equivalent enforcement; provider outages may prevent the next exchange; (d) keeps them signed in for the token's life even after a revocation that the session row has not caught. Operators: (a) adds one migration and an issuer role; (c) changes the serving role's grants. Multitenant only.

    Lifetimes. Those already stated: 10 min / 30 min / 12 h / 5 min / 8 h (owner-authority.md:97-102); 3600 s was a lab value, not a policy; 28800 s is the data cap in code. Values the maintainer must set, each with a recommended default and its basis:

    value recommended default basis
    refresh-secret reuse grace (a second presentation of the just-rotated secret within this window is a retry, not an attack) 30 s the cookie-variants record notes E's assertion was valid "for at most 30 s at the backend" (§6); this assertion lifetime does not establish a safe refresh retry window. Retry latency and concurrent refresh measurements must set the bound. Unmeasured; no approved default
    management session absolute lifetime for headless management credentials (operator-provisioned, owner-authority.md:89-93) same 12 h, renewable without a human the contract says unattended uploaders renew without a human login; headless management should not be looser than human
    maximum concurrent management sessions per (tenant, subject) 5 an owner on a phone, a laptop and a caregiver's browser; bounds the family-revocation blast radius. Unmeasured
    management-token clock skew tolerance 0 (none) access-token.js:51 already refuses iat > now; the lab ran in UTC with synchronised clocks; add tolerance only if a measurement shows refusals

    Recommendation: (a). Direction: it is the contract's primary form and keeps "provider sessions do not certify grants" true in code. Architecture: it preserves R48's writer/reader split, which is what made the read-only serving identity possible. Compatibility: device tokens and single are untouched. Maintenance: one table extension in our own candidate. Reliability: the only outage that ends a session early is PostgreSQL, which already refuses every protected operation. Performance: refresh and qualifying-activity writes require measurement; no per-session write rate is established. Unmeasured: the refresh endpoint, concurrent rotation, per-operation idle enforcement and qualifying-activity propagation; each requires an entry-bar control in §3.

    Q3. Issuer and audience split; key binding and rotation¶

    Mechanism. D14: per-tenant signing key, resolved after tenant resolution. D16: three listeners; ns-tenant-admin is a second bound socket inside ns-api (decided 2026-10-16). D22 as amended 2026-11-05 and the contract's Vault amendment (owner-authority.md:110-122): private keys stay in the secrets component; serving workers verify with provisioned public keys; "verification pins algorithm, issuer, audience, tenant, purpose and key binding ... rejects unknown keys. Key discovery never follows a request-supplied URL/JWK."     Options.

    • (b) ns-tenant-admin is the issuer: the owner socket holds the management signing capability for every tenant it serves.
    • (c) The provider issues the management token (Hydra-style): the owner listener verifies the provider's token directly.
    • (d) One key per tenant for both purposes, purpose carried only by aud.

Compatibility: the R48 verifier needs no change, only a second configured purpose. Maintenance: one issuer, two key sets. Reliability: issuer outage degrades to "no new sessions"; verification continues. Performance: one internal hop per login or refresh, not per operation. Unmeasured: the issuer role itself, full cross-purpose key-isolation acceptance and rotation overlap/cutoff. The bounded newer owner wrong-purpose refusal is already measured as described above; it is not a human-issuer proof.

    Q4. Existing-grant authority: withdrawn reader mode, future M6 research¶

    Decided: withdraw the optional subject-reader clamping mode. The promotion-bar decision at alignment 56951c8088ec7f942c875a52a13f8816bbafea8f supersedes this packet's earlier per-tenant activation/preflight proposal and the earlier root-carrier →401 recommendation. Changing that refusal from503 to401 would still break compatibility. A tenant's own API_SECRET must continue to get 200 on its own protected resource. There is no retained reader selection, rollout preflight or opt-in mode recommended here.

    Historical evidence stays valid at its executed scope. R62 measured the dormant reader's intersection and controls; R63 measured preserved subject-token uploads and an incompatible root-carrier refusal under selection, against a successful unselected reference. Those records explain the withdrawal; they are not acceptance of a current mode. Their measurements remain in the integration record (R62/R63); this correction does not rewrite them. Local source candidate e2a760bc removes the reader-only factory/options, joins/intersections and reader-only cases. That is source removal, not a new client execution result.

    Retained writer policy is separate. R53's management transaction still constrains proposed subject grants to the reviewed catalogue, current operator ceiling and current actor's delegation (lib/subject-admin/owner-policy.js createOwnerPolicy). The headless owner's ownerPolicy configuration feeds that writer only. It does not alter data-reader authority or promote root/data credentials to management authority.

    Future M6 research, not an implementation selection. Existing-grant lifecycle ideas must preserve existing credentials and first specify provenance, effective-authority transitions and recovery semantics. No sweep, per-tenant switch, replacement mode or root refusal is adopted here. Any proposal needs actual legacy root/API_SECRET and subject-client compatibility execution, cross-tenant refusal, current revocation and no-effect oracles, with independently calibrated controls. A subject-only pass cannot establish backward compatibility. Thresholds, activation policy, migration and performance remain unmeasured; the current decision is to remove the mode.

    Q5. Ownership lifecycle¶

    Preview scope decided: operator-assisted ownership changes are sufficient initially. Self-service co-owner/transfer proposals below are later enhancements, not first-preview gates. The operator path still needs current authority, last-owner protection, audit and recovery without secret disclosure. The earlier recommendation (a) below is retained as a future proposal, not the adopted preview implementation. The later deletion ceremony decision requires explicit request and operator confirmation, cancellation until execution starts, and no mandatory waiting period. It supersedes the proposed 14-day pre-execution window below. The adopted 30-day post-deletion key/artifact retention remains separate; other future invitation/self-service proposals are not adopted.

    Later decision, separate from this draft's recommendations: the maintainer's promotion-bar decision record adopts retaining Transit keys and the export artifact for 30 days, followed by operator key destruction. R69 measures recovery while laboratory keys remain; key destruction and restore-after-window refusal remain unmeasured. This artifact retention window is separate from the proposed cancellation/confirmation window below. Other lifecycle proposals in this packet are not adopted by that decision.

    Mechanism. The contract (owner-authority.md:38-48): "Owner is an explicit tenant relationship, separate from legacy data wildcards"; initial capabilities include "export and deletion requests"; "Operator provisioning, suspension, policy changes and execution of lifecycle deletion remain on the private operator plane"; "Secret reveal is a separate permission with recent reauthentication. Ownership transfer and adding co-owners are sensitive explicit operations; last-owner removal must not orphan the tenant. Transactional transfer/recovery details require a bounded implementation contract, not automatic operator access to owner secrets." The roadmap (:648-652): "an owner may export or request deletion; the operator authorizes and executes lifecycle deletion. A deletion request is not permission to bypass confirmation, retention or recovery policy." D7: the operator plane is credential-free by unreachability. In code, the only owner-identity table is tenant_owner_actors(principal, consumer, active, can_update, delegable) (migration 0004), read FOR SHARE and never written by the application; the subject-admin audit records subject mutations only; the platform authority spec §8 lists "who took an owner action and when" as not recorded.

    Measured. R53's actor fencing (an inactive or non-matching actor refuses, 0 / 1 control); the secrets component's reveal permission and audit (34-case suite). Read: everything else in this question.

    A contradiction to resolve inside this question. There are two owner-identity models and no mapping between them: tenant_owner_actors keys an actor by (principal, consumer) (0004, built and measured in R53), while the schema-config lab keys a person by (provider, subject_id) in tenant_access_grants (schema spec Q10/Q18, maintainer notes 2026-10-29, "one access-grant contract, two stores"). Q1's exchange produces a (provider, subject); R53's policy consumes a (principal, consumer). The decision row for Q5 must say which table is the owner relationship of record and how the other is derived; the recommendation below takes tenant_access_grants as the relationship and tenant_owner_actors as its projection for the management writer.

    Options.

    • (a) Owner-plane operations on the owner listener, operator execution on the operator plane, one transaction each, one audit shape. Add co-owner = insert an access grant with the owner permission set, by an actor whose delegable covers it, with reauthentication ≤ 5 min. Transfer = two steps: the current owner records an invitation naming a (provider, subject); the invitee's own verified session accepts it; the old owner's grant is kept until acceptance and removed in the accepting transaction. Remove owner (self or co-owner) = refused in the same transaction when the count of remaining active owner grants FOR UPDATE would be zero (last-owner invariant). Recovery when the only owner is locked out = an operator-plane command that writes a fresh owner invitation for a named (provider, subject) and records who ran it; it never reveals, decrypts or reads a secret (contract :47-48). Deletion = an owner writes a tenant_lifecycle_requests row (requested, with reauthentication); the operator plane moves it to scheduled after the retention window and executed by the export-then-delete tooling the Batch C lane is building; the owner may cancel until scheduled. Every step writes the same audit record shape on its plane.
    • (b) Operator-only ownership: co-owner, transfer, removal and recovery all run on the operator plane by ticket; owners request them out of band.
    • (c) Owner-plane everything, including deletion execution, with a confirmation step.
    • (d) Single owner per tenant, no co-owners; transfer and recovery are operator-only.
